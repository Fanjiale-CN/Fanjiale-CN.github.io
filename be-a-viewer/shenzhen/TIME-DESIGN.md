# Shenzhen Time Layer — interaction design

This layer extends the existing `DESIGN.md`; it does not replace it.

## Core idea

Shenzhen is read on two time scales. The existing page captures one day through five city frequencies. The new layer adds decades: **ROOTS → GROWTH → TODAY**. The user first sees what existed before the modern metropolis, then physically scrolls through the city’s expansion, then lands in the existing present-day field note.

## Visual identity

Use the exact Shenzhen palette already established:

- Ink `#0a1012`
- Warm paper `#eeeae0`
- Signal red `#e14b3b`
- Tidal cyan `#77b9bd`
- Sodium amber `#ef9e45`

ROOTS stays on warm paper and feels archival. GROWTH enters the ink field and behaves like a control room / satellite readout. TODAY returns to the original frequency system.

## Motion model

The hero end state and existing five-frequency composition remain unchanged. New motion is layered before them.

- Native scrolling stays authoritative.
- The growth visual is sticky; the reading column moves.
- NASA Landsat time is scrubbed from 1973 to 2001.
- Later photographs crossfade in-place without moving the layout.
- Active years alter readout text, source credit and image layer.
- Mobile uses discrete year seeking and shorter steps.
- Reduced-motion users receive static milestone states.

## Image treatment

Historical cards never force a decorative crop. Every archival image is contained inside a neutral media field, so portrait documents, panoramas and low-resolution scans retain their complete frame.

## What not to do

- Do not turn the archive into a generic masonry gallery.
- Do not use fake AI-upscaled historical imagery.
- Do not repeat the “30,000-person fishing village” claim as a citywide statistic.
- Do not replace the existing five-frequency present-day narrative.
- Do not add WebGL merely for spectacle; the growth sequence must remain readable on iPhone and iPad.
