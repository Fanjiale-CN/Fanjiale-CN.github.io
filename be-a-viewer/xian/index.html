01 / INTRODUCTION

Introduction

Xi’an is one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in China. Known in earlier periods as Chang’an, it served as the capital of several Chinese dynasties and stood at the eastern end of the ancient Silk Roads.

Its history is not contained in one monument. Palace sites, temple precincts, city gates and working streets overlap across the modern city, making Xi’an feel less like a preserved stage set than a place built in visible layers.

02 / IMPERIAL ORIGIN

The First Capital

Near modern Xi’an, Qin Shi Huang established the first unified imperial state in Chinese history in 221 BCE. The political institutions created during the Qin period shaped the structure of Chinese government for centuries.

Later dynasties returned to the Wei River plain. Under the Han and Tang, Chang’an became an imperial metropolis connected through the Silk Roads to Central Asia and beyond. Goods travelled with beliefs, languages, music and technologies, turning the capital into a meeting place for distant worlds.

03 / ARCHAEOLOGY

Terracotta Army

Discovered in 1974 near the mausoleum of Qin Shi Huang, the Terracotta Army consists of thousands of life-sized soldiers, horses and chariots. Their faces, hairstyles, armour and postures vary, giving the army an extraordinary sense of individuality.

The pits are also an archive of Qin military organisation and technical craft. Uniforms, weapons, chariot fittings and the arrangement of the figures preserve evidence of how the first empire represented order—at the scale of an army.

04 / URBAN FORM

City Wall

Xi’an’s surviving city wall was expanded during the early Ming dynasty on older imperial foundations. Its complete 13.74-kilometre circuit still encloses the historic centre and remains one of China’s best-preserved major city walls.

From the rampart, defensive geometry meets apartment towers, traffic and trees. The wall is both boundary and viewpoint: a continuous line from which the old and new city can be read together.

05 / THE CITY NOW

Xi’an Today

Modern Xi’an is a major university, research, aerospace and technology centre. High-speed rail and an expanding metro system connect the historic capital with the rest of China, while its neighbourhoods retain strong food and street-life traditions.

At the centre, the Bell and Drum Towers still organise movement through the city. Farther south, the Big Wild Goose Pagoda recalls the Buddhist texts and ideas that travelled into Chang’an. These monuments are not isolated from daily life; they remain anchors within it.
