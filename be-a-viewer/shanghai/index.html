01 / THE HUANGPU

THE RIVER
AS ADDRESS

31.2304° N / 121.4737° E

Shanghai introduces itself across water. One bank keeps the stone façades of trade; the other answers in steel, glass and light.

The Huangpu is not a dividing line. It is the city’s longest room—a place where every period turns to face the next.

02 / PUDONG

VERTICAL
CITY

A skyline built as a declaration.
The city does not hide its ambition.

In Pudong, towers become instruments for measuring weather. Their crowns vanish, return and rearrange the scale of the street below.

03 / STREET VELOCITY

CITY IN
MOTION

Movement gives the plan its meaning.

Shanghai is read at two speeds: the long view from the river and the quick cut of a crossing. Between them, the city is constantly edited by commuters, deliveries, bicycles and light.

04 / NEW ARCHITECTURE

NEW
FORMS

Buildings shaped as public encounters.

Shanghai’s new architecture is most convincing when spectacle becomes useful: a stair becomes a theatre, a roof becomes a walk, a façade frames a gathering.

05 / EVERYDAY FABRIC

STREETS
REMEMBER

The old city survives through use, not silence.
History here is rarely alone.

Plane trees, campus walls, shopfronts and apartment windows keep different Shanghais in the same field of view. Memory persists because the street keeps moving through it.

06 / AFTER DARK

LIGHT BECOMES
ARCHITECTURE

The city redraws itself every evening.

After dark, infrastructure becomes theatre. Headlights trace the elevated roads; towers turn into signals; the river carries the colour without keeping it.
