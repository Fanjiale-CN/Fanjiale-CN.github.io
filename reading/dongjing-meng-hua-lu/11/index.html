DONGJING MENG HUA LU / 卷二

東角樓街巷

Dongjiaolou Streets

This entry does more than list shops. Around Panlou, the market changes by time of day: valuables before dawn, fresh meat at first light, workshop supplies later, sweets after meals, personal goods toward evening — then the wazi keeps people there until dusk.

11 / 東角樓街巷

Entry 11 is built around two structures: high-value trade at Jieshen, then a market whose inventory changes through the day.← READING ROOM

Meng begins with a famously busy shopping district east of the palace, then moves into Pearl Street, Jieshen and Panlou. The chapter becomes most revealing when place and time overlap: one neighborhood carries luxury trade, food supply, workshop inputs, sweets, personal goods and entertainment without needing separate modern zones.

1. East of the palace, retail concentration becomes a landmark.

PRIMARY TEXT11A / EAST OF THE PALACE
自宣德東去，東角樓，乃皇城東南角也。十字街南去姜行。高頭街北去，從紗行至東華門街、晨暉門、寶籙宮，直至舊酸棗門，最是鋪席要鬧。宣和間展夾城牙道矣。東去乃潘樓街，街南曰鷹店，只下販鷹鶻客，餘皆真珠疋帛、香藥鋪席。
WORKING TRANSLATION

East from Xuande Gate stood Dongjiaolou at the southeast corner of the imperial city. Streets branched south and north through the ginger and gauze trades toward Donghua Gate, Chenhui Gate, Baolu Palace and the old Suanzao Gate; this was one of the city's busiest concentrations of shops. During the Xuanhe period the covered imperial-side roadway was extended. Farther east was Panlou Street. South of it stood an “eagle inn” used by hawk traders; the remaining shops dealt largely in pearls, bolts of silk and aromatic drugs.

HIGH-VALUE TRADE / 界身

界身

Jieshen is best kept in its own historical name. The text describes a concentration of gold, silver and luxury textile exchange in imposing buildings. That is already strong evidence; calling it a “Song Wall Street” would import a modern institution the source does not describe.

金銀綵帛交易之所A place for trading gold, silver and colored silks.
每一交易，動即千萬Meng emphasizes very large transactions; the phrase is not converted into modern money.
PRIMARY TEXT11B / JIESHEN
南通一巷，謂之界身，並是金銀綵帛交易之所。屋宇雄壯，門面廣闊，望之森然。每一交易，動即千萬，駭人聞見。
WORKING TRANSLATION

A lane running south was called Jieshen. It was a place where gold, silver and colored silks were traded. The buildings were imposing, their shopfronts broad and formidable in appearance. Meng says that individual transactions could readily reach extremely large sums, enough to astonish those who heard of them.

Siku text / Wikisource ↗Translation: Galok / working translation
GALOK READING

What matters here is concentration: precious metals, luxury textiles and large transactions occupy a recognizable urban node with purpose-built commercial frontage. The evidence is strong enough to call it a high-value trading district and stops there.

2. Panlou records a market in motion, not a static category.

PRIMARY TEXT11C / MARKET CLOCK
以東街北曰潘樓酒店，其下每日自五更市合，買賣衣物、書畫、珍玩、犀玉。至平明，羊頭、肚肺、赤白腰子、妳房、肚胘、鵪兔、鳩鴿、野味、螃蟹、蛤蜊之類訖，方有諸手作人上市買賣零碎作料。飯後飲食上市，如酥蜜食、棗䬴、澄沙團子、香糖果子、蜜煎雕花之類。向晚賣河婁頭面、冠梳領抹、珍玩動使之類。
WORKING TRANSLATION

On the north side of the street stood Panlou wine house. Beneath it, a market assembled every day from the fifth watch, trading clothing, paintings and calligraphy, curios, rhinoceros horn and jade. By first light the goods had shifted to sheep heads, offal, kidneys, named cuts, quail, rabbit, pigeons, game, crabs and shellfish. After that, craft workers came to buy and sell small materials. After meals, prepared foods and sweets entered the market: pastries, filled dumplings, scented sugar confections and carved honeyed preserves. Toward evening, vendors sold head ornaments, caps, combs, collars, curios and everyday objects.

Siku text / Wikisource ↗Translation: Galok / working translation
GALOK READING

The source clearly records time-of-day replacement of goods and users. It does not tell us that officials designed a formal “time-sharing market system.” The safer claim is simply that the same market space displayed strong temporal specialization.

TEXT NOTE / FOOD AND OBJECT NAMES

Several names in this list vary across witnesses or are difficult to identify securely, including 妳房、肚胘、棗䬴、河婁頭面. The translation therefore preserves the sequence without pretending every commodity can be reconstructed precisely.

METHOD / TIME

Five watches, dawn, after-meal and evening markers come from the source. The visual clock is editorial.

界身 / LIMIT

High-value trade is secure. Modern labels such as stock exchange or Wall Street are deliberately avoided.

Entry 12 continues east from Panlou into another dense network of streets, tea shops, night markets and entertainment quarters.

02 / WAZI

The street expands into an entertainment-service network.

Sangjia Wazi, Zhongwa and Liwa contained more than fifty performance enclosures, but the text also places medicine sellers, fortune tellers, second-hand clothes, gambling-like games, food, hair cutting, prints and music inside the same cluster.

PRIMARY TEXT11D / WAZI
東去則徐家瓠羹店。街南桑家瓦子，近北則中瓦，次里瓦。其中大小勾欄五十餘座。內中瓦子蓮花棚、牡丹棚，里瓦子夜叉棚、象棚最大，可容數千人。自丁先現、王團子、張七聖輩，後來可有人於此作場。瓦中多有貨藥、賣卦、喝故衣、探摶、飲食、剃剪、紙畫、令曲之類。終日居此，不覺抵暮。
WORKING TRANSLATION

Farther east stood the Xu family's gourd-soup shop. South of the street were Sangjia Wazi, then Zhongwa and Liwa. Together they contained more than fifty large and small performance enclosures. The largest named sheds could hold crowds in the thousands. Performers appeared here, while the wazi also contained medicine sellers, fortune tellers, used-clothing hawkers, games, food, hair cutting, paper pictures and music. One could spend the whole day here and reach dusk without noticing.
