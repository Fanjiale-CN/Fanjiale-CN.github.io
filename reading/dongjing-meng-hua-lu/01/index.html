DONGJING MENG HUA LU / 卷一

東都外城

Outer City

Meng Yuanlao begins his lost capital at the edge: moat, walls, barbicans, gates, rivers, military stores and the roads reserved for imperial movement. Before markets and festivals appear, the city is first described as a managed boundary.

READ ENTRY ↓

01 / 東都外城

The complete first entry. The book opens with the outer city's defensive and hydraulic frame before moving inward.← READING ROOM

Dongdu Waicheng is almost administrative in tone. The first thing Meng Yuanlao remembers is not a famous shop or a festival but the capital's perimeter. His description ties security, ceremonial movement, river traffic and routine maintenance into the same urban system.

1. The capital begins with a controlled edge.

PRIMARY TEXT / 東都外城01A / WALL + MOAT
東都外城，方圓四十餘里。城壕曰護龍河，闊十餘丈，濠之內外，皆植楊柳，粉牆朱戶，禁人往來。城門皆甕城三層，屈曲開門；唯南薰門、新鄭門、新宋門、封丘門皆直門兩重，蓋此係四正門，皆留御路故也。
WORKING TRANSLATION

The outer city of the Eastern Capital extended for more than forty li around. Its moat was called the Hulong River and was more than ten zhang wide. Willows stood along both sides, with white walls and red gates, and ordinary passage was forbidden. Most city gates were protected by three layers of barbican walls and entered through turning approaches. Four principal gates — Nanxun, Xinzheng, Xinsong and Fengqiu — instead used two straight gate lines because imperial roads were kept through them.

Wikisource / Volume I ↗Translation: Galok / working translation
GALOK READING

The opening description is a system of access control. The moat is wide, its margins are restricted, and the ordinary gate geometry forces movement through bends. A wall is only one part of the defense; the approach itself is designed to slow and channel entry.

The four principal gates expose a second logic layered onto defense. They stay straighter because the imperial road requires a ceremonial axis. Security and sovereign movement therefore share the same infrastructure, and one has to bend when the other demands a clear line.

2. Gate names become a map of the capital's connections.

PRIMARY TEXT01B / SOUTH + EAST
新城南壁，其門有三：正南門曰南薰門；城南一邊，東南則陳州門，傍有蔡河水門；西南則戴樓門，傍亦有蔡河水門。蔡河正名惠民河，為通蔡州故也。東城一邊，其門有四：東南曰東水門，乃汴河下流水門也，其門跨河，有鐵裹窗門，遇夜如閘垂下水面，兩岸各有門通人行路，出拐子城，夾岸百餘丈；次則曰新宋門；次曰新曹門；又次曰東北水門，乃五丈河之水門也。
WORKING TRANSLATION

Meng then walks the perimeter. On the south wall he names Nanxun Gate, Chenzhou Gate with a Cai River water gate, and Dailou Gate with another. The Cai River was formally the Huimin River, named here through its connection toward Caizhou. On the east wall he lists the eastern water gate, where the Bian River left the city. That gate crossed the river and could lower an iron-covered barrier to the water at night, while separate passages on both banks carried people through. Xinsong Gate, Xincao Gate and the northeast water gate on the Wuzhang River follow.

Wikisource / Volume I ↗Translation: Galok / working translation
GALOK READING

The wall is repeatedly punctured by water. That makes the boundary harder to understand as a simple military line. Rivers have to pass through the fortification because the capital depends on them for movement and supply.

The eastern water gate is particularly revealing: boats and water pass through, a barrier can close at night, and pedestrians use paths beside the river. One structure handles circulation and control at once. The city is not choosing between openness and closure; it is engineering adjustable gates between the two.

MEASURE / KEEP ORIGINAL UNITS

Meng gives the outer perimeter as 40+ li and the moat as 10+ zhang wide. Galok keeps the source units here rather than pretending a single modern conversion is exact.

TEXT / 1147 MEMORY

The book was completed after the Northern Song capital had been lost. The prose records remembered urban detail, so archaeological and visual evidence are used as checks rather than automatic confirmation.

VISUAL WITNESS / CITY GATE

A Song-period city-gate image helps us see the architectural problem. It does not settle which named Kaifeng gate the painting depicts.

3. The western and northern gates connect defense to regional movement.

PRIMARY TEXT01C / WEST + NORTH
西城一邊，其門有四：從南曰新鄭門；次曰西水門，汴河上水門也；次曰萬勝門；又次曰固子門；又次曰西北水門，乃金水河水門也。北城一邊，其門有四：從東曰陳橋門（乃大遼人使驛路）；次曰封丘門（北郊御路）；次曰新酸棗門；次曰衞州門。（諸門名皆俗呼。其正名如西水門曰利澤，鄭門本順天門，固子門本金耀門。）
WORKING TRANSLATION

On the west, Meng lists Xinzheng Gate, the western Bian River water gate, Wansheng Gate, Guzi Gate and the northwest water gate on the Jinshui River. On the north, he names Chenqiao Gate — the post road used by envoys from the Liao — Fengqiu Gate on the imperial road toward the northern suburbs, Xinsuanzao Gate and Weizhou Gate. He adds that these are common names; several gates had different formal names.

Wikisource / Volume I ↗Translation: Galok / working translation
GALOK READING

Now the gates become geopolitical infrastructure. Chenqiao is remembered through the route taken by Liao envoys; Fengqiu through the imperial road to the northern suburbs. A gate's meaning comes partly from what lies beyond it.

The note about common and formal names also tells us how urban memory works. Administrative naming and everyday naming coexist. Meng is preserving the city as people called it, then occasionally stopping to correct the record.

4. A monumental wall survives through repetitive maintenance.

PRIMARY TEXT01D / MAINTENANCE
新城每百步設馬面、戰棚，密置女頭，旦暮修整，望之聳然。城裏牙道，各植榆柳成陰。每二百步置一防城庫，貯守禦之器，有廣固兵士二十指揮，每日修造泥飾，專有京城所提總其事。
WORKING TRANSLATION

Along the new wall, defensive projections, fighting shelters and battlements appeared at regular intervals and were repaired morning and evening. Roads inside the wall were shaded with elms and willows. Every two hundred paces stood a defense storehouse containing equipment for guarding the city. Guanggu troops, organized in twenty commands, worked daily on repairs and plastering under an office responsible for the capital's fortifications.

Wikisource / Volume I ↗Translation: Galok / working translation
GALOK READING

The final paragraph changes the scale from monument to routine. The wall looks imposing because people are constantly repairing it. Stores are spaced along the perimeter, equipment is kept close to where it would be used, and a dedicated organization coordinates the work.

That matters for how the rest of the book should be read. Meng Yuanlao's Kaifeng is full of spectacular consumption, but even the first page shows the less glamorous systems underneath it: maintenance labor, storage, patrol infrastructure, river control and administrative attention. Urban splendor rests on repeated work.

VISUAL LIMIT

The famous handscroll is not a cadastral map. Palace Museum research has argued that the painted gate deliberately avoids a firm real-world identification and does not perfectly match the textual description of the traditionally proposed site.
