02 / WHAT IS 陌?

A nominal hundred can circulate with fewer than one hundred coins.

Traditional lexicographic and monetary-history sources describe 省陌／短陌 as practices in which a count below one hundred is treated as a nominal hundred. The official 77-count convention is a particularly important reference point.

HISTORICAL FRAME足陌 = full hundred
省陌 / 短陌 = shortened count used as a nominal hundred

That frame makes Meng’s last sentence — 「行市各有長短使用」 — legible as a system of transaction-specific counting conventions. It does not by itself tell us a modern equivalent value.

WORKING TRANSLATION

For money counting in the capital, Meng gives different actual counts by context: official use 77, common street-market use 75, fish/meat/vegetables 72, gold and silver 74, pearls and other valuables together with hiring female servants and buying insects 68, and written-document transactions 56. He closes by saying trades use conventions of different lengths.

GALOK READING

The chapter is a warning against treating a written monetary unit as a single physical count. The transaction category changes the counting convention. The page therefore shows counts and categories directly and avoids converting them to percentages, discounts, exchange rates or modern currency.

INTERPRETIVE LIMIT / 陌COUNT IT. DO NOT MODERNIZE IT.

The figures stay in their source context. “77 of a nominal 100” is a historical counting convention; this page does not infer a 23% discount or any modern purchasing-power equivalent.
