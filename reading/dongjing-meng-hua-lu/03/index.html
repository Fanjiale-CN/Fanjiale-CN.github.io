DONGJING MENG HUA LU / 卷一

河道

Waterways

Four rivers cross the capital. Meng Yuanlao follows them through water gates and under bridges, from regional grain routes to the imperial avenue and finally into palace gardens. Here the city stops looking like a set of walls and starts behaving like a network.

READ ENTRY ↓

03 / 河道

Four river systems turn the capital into a logistics machine: Huimin/Cai, Bian, Wuzhang and Jinshui.← READING ROOM

Hedao is one of Volume I's most revealing entries. Meng inventories bridges with the same attention he gave gates. The names can feel relentless until the pattern appears: each river has a different job, and the capital depends on all of them.

1. The southern river stitches the city to regional routes.

PRIMARY TEXT / 河道03A / 蔡河 · 惠民河
穿城河道有四。南壁曰蔡河，自陳蔡由西南戴樓門入京城，遼繞自東南陳州門出。河上有橋十一：自陳州門裏曰觀橋（在五嶽觀後門），從北，次曰宣泰橋，次曰雲騎橋，次曰橫橋子（在彭婆婆宅前），次曰高橋，次曰西保康門橋，次曰龍津橋（正對內前），次曰新橋，次曰太平橋（高殿前宅前），次曰糶麥橋，次曰第一座橋，次曰宜男橋，出戴樓門外曰四里橋。
WORKING TRANSLATION

Four waterways crossed the city. On the southern side ran the Cai River, which entered from the southwest through Dailou Gate after coming from the Chen and Cai region, wound across the capital and left through Chenzhou Gate in the southeast. Meng then counts the crossings one by one: Guan Bridge, Xuantai, Yunqi, Hengqiaozi, Gao Bridge, West Baokang Gate Bridge, Longjin, Xin Bridge, Taiping, Tiaomai, First Bridge and Yinan, with Sili Bridge beyond Dailou Gate.

Wikisource / Volume I ↗Translation: Galok / working translation
GALOK READING

The list of bridges is the point. A river that repeatedly meets streets becomes part of the street network itself. Meng is mapping where water and land traffic collide, cross and exchange.

The earlier outer-city entry called this same watercourse the Huimin River and explained its Cai River name through the route toward Caizhou. The city remembers infrastructure by destination as much as by official nomenclature.

2. The Bian River is the capital's supply line.

PRIMARY TEXT03B / 汴河 · GRAIN + GOODS
中曰汴河，自西京洛口分水入京城，東去至泗州，入淮。運東南之糧，凡東南方物，自此入京城，公私仰給焉。
WORKING TRANSLATION

Through the middle ran the Bian River. It drew water from the Luo River system near the Western Capital, entered Kaifeng, then flowed east toward Sizhou and the Huai. Grain from the southeast and goods of every kind from that region entered the capital by this route. Public institutions and private households alike depended on it for supply.

Chinese Text Project / Volume I ↗Textual comparison / river entry
GALOK READING

“Public and private alike depended on it” is the economic core of the entry. The Bian River is not decorative urban water. It links the capital's consumption to the lower Yangtze and Huai regions and makes a huge inland city materially possible.

This is also why the river dominates visual memory of Kaifeng. The commercial density around boats, warehouses, bridges and riverbank streets in Along the River During the Qingming Festival matches the kind of urban pressure Meng's sentence implies, even though painting and text should not be treated as one-to-one illustrations of each other.

FUNCTION / BIAN RIVER

Meng explicitly ties the Bian River to southeastern grain and goods. This makes the river a fiscal and subsistence artery, not simply a route through town.

READING / BRIDGE LISTS

The repetitive names are useful data. Bridges mark points where the river network intersects streets, temples, residences, warehouses and the imperial avenue.

VISUAL WITNESS / BIAN RIVER

The Qingming handscroll turns the logistical river into a crowded visual system: cargo boats, riverbank work, shops and the famous timber arch bridge. It is used here as an independent visual witness to Song urban river life.

3. Hongqiao solves one problem; Zhouqiao creates another.

PRIMARY TEXT03C / 汴河 · BRIDGES
自東水門外七里至西水門外，河上有橋十三：從東水門外七里曰虹橋，其橋無柱，皆以巨木虛架，飾以丹雘，宛如飛虹，其上下土橋亦如之；次曰順成倉橋，入水門裏曰便橋，次曰下土橋，次曰上土橋，投西角子門曰相國寺橋。
WORKING TRANSLATION

From seven li beyond the eastern water gate to the western water gate, Meng counts thirteen bridges. Hongqiao stood outside the east gate: a pierless span built from great timbers and painted in red, arcing “like a flying rainbow.” Shuncheng Granary Bridge followed; inside the water gate came Bian Bridge, Lower Earth Bridge, Upper Earth Bridge and Xiangguo Temple Bridge.

Palace Museum / timber arch bridge study ↗Visual + structural comparison
GALOK READING

A pierless timber bridge leaves the navigation channel open beneath it. That is an engineering response to a river carrying serious traffic. The bridge has to connect the two banks without choking the route that makes the banks valuable.

The Palace Museum's research treats the timber arch in the Qingming scroll as a real Song bridge type rather than a painter's fantasy. That supports the plausibility of Meng's “great timbers” and pierless span while still leaving exact bridge identification open to debate.

PRIMARY TEXT03D / 州橋 · IMPERIAL AVENUE
次曰州橋（正名天漢橋），正對於大內御街，其橋與相國寺橋皆低平不通舟船，唯西河平船可過，其柱皆青石為之，石梁石筍楯欄。近橋兩岸，皆石壁，雕鐫海馬水獸飛雲之狀。橋下密排石柱，蓋車駕御路也。
WORKING TRANSLATION

Next came Zhouqiao, formally Tianhan Bridge, aligned directly with the imperial avenue before the palace. Zhouqiao and Xiangguo Temple Bridge were low enough to obstruct ordinary boats; only low, flat craft could pass. Zhouqiao used green-stone supports, stone beams and balustrades. Stone revetments on the nearby banks were carved with sea-horses, aquatic beasts and flying clouds, while closely spaced stone piers beneath the crossing supported the route used by the imperial carriage.

Henan Institute of Cultural Relics and Archaeology / Zhouqiao ↗Excavated urban anchor
ARCHAEOLOGY / TEXT MEETS GROUND

Zhouqiao is where this chapter becomes unusually testable. Excavation has located the bridge and adjacent Bian River at the crossing of the imperial avenue and canal. Archaeologists exposed Tang–Song riverbanks and later bridge remains, establishing a fixed coordinate for reconstructing the capital.

The excavated Song-period stone revetments carry large carved compositions of auspicious animals, cranes and clouds. Meng remembered carved “sea-horses, water beasts and flying clouds” along the banks. The match is not a photograph of the sentence — the surviving fabric has its own chronology and interpretation — but it is a striking material echo of the kind of monumental stonework he described.

PRIMARY TEXT03E / 汴河 · NIGHT CONTROL + WESTERN BRIDGES
州橋之北岸御路，東西兩闕，樓觀對聳；橋之西有方淺舡二隻，頭置巨杆鐵鎗數條，岸上有鐵索三條，遇夜絞上水面，蓋防遺失舟船矣。西去曰浚儀橋，次曰興國寺橋（亦名馬軍衙橋），次曰太師府橋（蔡相宅前），次曰金梁橋，次曰西浮橋（舊以船為之橋，今皆用木石造矣），次曰西水門便橋，門外曰橫橋。
WORKING TRANSLATION

North of Zhouqiao, towers rose on both sides of the imperial road. West of the bridge lay two shallow guard boats equipped with long poles and iron-tipped gear; three iron chains on the bank could be winched across the water at night to stop boats drifting or disappearing. Farther west Meng lists Junyi, Xingguo Temple, Grand Preceptor's Residence, Jinliang, West Floating Bridge, the western water-gate bridge and Hengqiao beyond the gate. He notes that the old floating bridge had already been rebuilt in timber and stone.

Wikisource / Volume I ↗Translation: Galok / working translation
GALOK READING

The river is open enough to feed the city and controlled enough to be closed at night. Chains, guard boats and low bridges show that logistics and policing occupy the same waterway.

Zhouqiao also exposes a conflict between ceremonial urban design and navigation. The imperial avenue wants a monumental, low, load-bearing crossing; commercial river traffic wants clearance. Meng records the compromise in physical form: ordinary boats cannot pass, while low craft still can.

4. Two more rivers specialize: grain in, palace water in.

PRIMARY TEXT03F / 五丈河
東北曰五丈河，來自濟、鄆，般挽京東路糧斛入京城。自新曹門北入京，河上有橋五：東去曰小橫橋，次曰廣備橋，次曰蔡市橋，次曰青暉橋、染院橋。
WORKING TRANSLATION

To the northeast ran the Wuzhang River, bringing grain from the Ji and Yun regions and the Jingdong circuit into the capital. It entered north of Xincao Gate. Meng names five bridges along it: Xiaoheng, Guangbei, Caishi, Qinghui and Ranyuan.

Chinese Text Project / Volume I ↗Textual comparison
GALOK READING

The Bian River is the headline route, but the Wuzhang River shows why a single-river story is too simple. Grain also arrives from the northeast. The capital's food supply is distributed across multiple corridors.

PRIMARY TEXT03G / 金水河 + 念佛橋
西北曰金水河，自京城西南分京、索河水築堤，從汴河上用木槽架過，從西北水門入京城，夾牆遮擁，入大內灌後苑池浦矣。河上有橋三：曰白虎橋、橫橋、五王宮橋之類。又曹門小河子橋曰念佛橋，蓋內諸司輦官、親事官之類，軍營皆在曹門，侵晨上直，有瞽者在橋上念經求化，得其名矣。
WORKING TRANSLATION

In the northwest, the Jinshui River was diverted from the Jing and Suo river system southwest of the capital. Meng describes it being carried in a timber trough over the Bian River, entering through the northwest water gate and then running between enclosing walls into the imperial compound to fill ponds and waterways in the rear gardens. He names Baihu, Heng and Wuwanggong bridges. He closes with a smaller bridge near Cao Gate called Nianfo Bridge: nearby military and palace-service personnel went on duty before dawn, while a blind man recited sutras there seeking alms, and the bridge took its name from that routine.

Wikisource / Volume I ↗Translation: Galok / working translation
GALOK READING

The Jinshui River has a different destination: the palace. Water is diverted, raised across another river and enclosed on its way into imperial gardens. The network is technically ambitious because different flows are being assigned different urban functions.

Then Meng ends with a tiny human detail. A bridge acquires its everyday name from a blind man chanting before dawn while workers and soldiers pass on their way to duty. After the engineering, grain and imperial infrastructure, the city suddenly becomes audible again.

FOUR RIVERS / FOUR JOBS

Huimin/Cai: southern regional route. Bian: southeastern grain and goods. Wuzhang: northeastern grain. Jinshui: controlled water into the imperial gardens.

ARCHAEOLOGY / ZHOUQIAO

Excavation since 2018 has fixed the location of Zhouqiao and the Bian River crossing, exposed riverbanks and monumental stone revetments, and made this one of the strongest material checkpoints in the book.

ARCHAEOLOGICAL CHECK / 州橋

Meng's bridge list is not floating in empty literary space. The excavated Zhouqiao/Bian River site now supplies a physical coordinate for the imperial avenue, river channel, revetments and the long afterlife of this crossing.
2018–2022 / EXCAVATION

The Henan archaeological team identifies Zhouqiao at the intersection of the Northern Song imperial avenue and the Bian River segment of the Grand Canal. Excavation exposed remains from multiple periods, including Tang–Song riverbanks and later bridge structures. The project was selected among China's top ten archaeological discoveries for 2022.

For Galok, the most useful result is methodological: a remembered urban coordinate in a twelfth-century text can now be compared with excavated stratigraphy, bridge fabric and river engineering. That turns Dongjing Meng Hua Lu from atmosphere into something closer to a testable city document.
