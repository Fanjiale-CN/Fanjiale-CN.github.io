### Hard editorial rule

Do **not** use the Siku reading merely because it looks more intelligible.

Before the public primary-text block is locked, compare at least one early witness (Yuan blockprint and/or old manuscript) at these loci. Until then the page may discuss the disagreement, but it must label it as unresolved.

## Secure source-derived content across compared witnesses

The following high-level structure is stable enough for design planning:

1. East-gate street retail includes headwear, belts, books and tea.
2. The surrounding lanes include sex-work locations and a lane associated with religious women's embroidery work.
3. The Little Sweetwater Lane area includes southern-food businesses.
4. Medicine and boot shops appear in the route sequence.
5. A Song-family drug shop is described with landscape paintings on both walls attributed to **Li Cheng**.
6. Government / institutional sites, a major guesthouse and a wine establishment remain embedded in the same eastward urban fabric.
7. Multiple Sweetwater Lanes organize part of the remembered route.

## Li Cheng module — what is safe now

Both the common text and the Siku / WYG family contain the attribution that the two walls of the Song-family drug shop carried landscapes painted by Li Cheng.

Safe public framing:

> A drug shop is remembered not only by what it sold, but by the landscapes attributed to Li Cheng on its walls.

Do not expand this into a claim about surviving paintings, exact composition, date, patronage or authenticity unless a specialist art-historical source supports it.

If imagery is later used, it must be a clearly labelled public-domain comparative work / historical witness, never presented as the lost shop murals themselves.
