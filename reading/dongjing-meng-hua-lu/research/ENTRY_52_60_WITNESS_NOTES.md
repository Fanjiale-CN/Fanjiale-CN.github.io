## 2. E54 `百姓卸在行人` — an important older scholarly interpretation

The text itself remains:

> 又有虎頭船十隻……餘皆着青短衣長頂頭巾，齊舞棹，乃百姓卸在行人也。

A significant older interpretation appears in **全漢昇《中國行會制度史》**, first published in Shanghai by 新生命書局 in 1934 (陶希聖校). In its discussion of Song guild dress / occupational differentiation, Quan reads `卸在行` as an occupational guild of wharf or land porters whose work involved unloading and carrying goods. He further suggests that `卸在` may reflect `卸載`.

This matters because the proposal comes from a foundational economic-history study focused specifically on Chinese guild organization, rather than a modern popular annotation.

### Current evaluation

The Quan Hansheng interpretation is now a **serious candidate reading**, but still not secure enough to erase the ambiguity:

- strength: historically informed specialist scholarship; fits an occupational `行` reading and the uniform dress/headgear context;
- weakness: the interpretation is itself an argument, and this pass has not located an independent Song source explicitly defining `卸在行` as porters;
- competing modern annotation reads the phrase differently, sometimes as people released from prior military / technical service.

### Updated status

`百姓卸在行人`:

- TEXT: **HIGH**
- candidate interpretation A: **卸在行 = unloading / carrying occupational guild (Quan Hansheng)** — MEDIUM
- candidate interpretation B: **former service personnel returned to civilian status** — LOW / MEDIUM-LOW
- final translation: remain cautious until an independent primary parallel is found.

A safe page apparatus can say:

> 全漢昇 interpreted `卸在行` as an occupational guild of wharf or land porters involved in unloading and carrying goods; other annotations read the label differently. The exact identification remains debated.

The main narrative should not silently replace the source label with “dockworkers”.

## 3. E52 `𣘚腳` — dictionary evidence strengthens the graph problem

The Ministry of Education Variant Dictionary records `𣘚` (shuài) as a **tree-name**, citing traditional rhyme-book material. This confirms that the standalone dictionary sense does not naturally explain the Qingming guard-scene phrase.

At the same time, modern / edited texts frequently print `摔腳`.

Research consequence:

- `𣘚` is not to be glossed from its dictionary tree-name sense in this context;
- `摔腳` remains a plausible textual normalization / variant because of phonetic proximity and the edited tradition;
- the meaning of the compound remains unresolved even if `摔` is accepted.

Status remains:

**TEXT VARIANT MEDIUM / MEANING LOW.**

The observable context is secure: grouped imperial guards ride out, make music, carry bright banners, and present a conspicuous military spectacle.

## 4. E53 `閉池` gains independent lexicographic support

The Ministry of Education's `典沒` entry quotes the *Dongjing Meng Hua Lu* passage as:

> 藝人勾肆，質庫，不以幾日解下，只至閉池，便典沒出賣。

and defines `典沒` as pledged goods being forfeited / disposed of when not redeemed within the agreed period.

Combined with Entry 60's explicit `自三月一日至四月八日閉池`, this makes `閉池` the strongest working reading for the commercial sentence.

Siku/Kanripo `閑池` remains a visible substantive variant.

## 5. E53 `面北臨水殿` — spatial convergence strengthens preferred reading

Architectural historian Marianna Shevchenko's reconstruction study quotes the passage as `有面北臨水殿` and integrates archaeological survey data placing a likely Linshui Hall foundation on the south bank of the Jinming Pool.

The south-bank location plus a north-facing hall coheres strongly with the water-spectacle layout described by Meng.

Status:

- preferred spatial / translation reading: **面北臨水殿**;
- Siku/Kanripo witness: **西北臨水殿**;
- apparatus required;
- image collation of the Yuan witness remains desirable before calling the textual issue closed.

The same reconstruction research also cautions that Meng's very large dimensions should not be converted into exact site geometry without uncertainty. This supports a relational map rather than a falsely precise GIS reconstruction.

## 6. E59 `出九、和合` — legal and miscellany evidence converges

Multiple strands now converge:

- legal language treats `出九` and `和合` as gambling-related culpable roles;
- 《能改齋漫錄》 records `出九入十` as terminology of gambling / `攤賭`;
- Song institutional material preserves `出九和合` in gambling-control cases;
- modern Song gambling scholarship explains `出九` as supplying / operating gambling implements and `和合` as gathering / arranging players.

Therefore the punctuation:

> 出九、和合，有名者任大頭、快活三之類。

is secure enough for the project.

`任大頭` and `快活三` are treated as named / nicknamed practitioners in this sentence. Wang Guowei later noted that `快活三` also survived as a Song-period expression associated with later tune nomenclature; that parallel usage does not turn Meng's `快活三` automatically into a tune title.

## 7. E54 contest count — `三次` has strong witness support

Kanripo / Siku-family text reads:

> 并虎頭船之類各三次爭標而止

A digitized Qing manuscript of 《幽蘭居士東京夢華錄》 likewise shows `各三次爭標而止`. Some modern annotated texts print `二次`.

Current decision:

- **working text: 三次**;
- `二次` stays as an edited variant if encountered;
- direct Yuan-edition image collation remains the ideal final check.

Do not transform the three contests into three identical heats unless the surrounding evidence supports that level of procedural reconstruction; the text only states the count.
