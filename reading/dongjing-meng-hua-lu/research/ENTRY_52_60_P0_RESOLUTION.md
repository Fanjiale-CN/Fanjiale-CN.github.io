## E52 — `𣘚腳` / `摔腳`

### Witness state

Kanripo / Siku-family witness transmits:

> 諸軍禁衛各成隊伍跨馬作樂四出謂之𣘚腳其旗旌鮮明軍容雄壯人馬精銳又别為一景也

A number of edited / modern digital texts print `摔腳`.

### Lexical check

The isolated graph `𣘚` is recorded in traditional dictionaries as a rare **tree-name**, with a reading close to *shuai*. That dictionary sense does not explain Meng's phrase. The phonetic proximity and modern edited form `摔腳` make graphic / phonetic normalization plausible, but the evidence inspected so far does not establish an early witness that decisively proves the emendation.

### Research decision

- Keep the source-facing apparatus as **`𣘚腳〔諸本／今本多作摔腳〕`**.
- Do not manufacture a precise modern gloss for the term.
- In translation / editorial prose, translate the observable action around it: guard units form groups, ride out on horseback, make music, and display banners / military appearance.
- The label itself can remain transliterated or bracketed as an unresolved historical term.

### Claim boundary

Safe:
- mounted guard groups went out in formation with music;
- Meng treats the scene as a distinct spectacle.

Unsafe without new evidence:
- a named dance form;
- a specific cavalry drill;
- a specific folk performance;
- a modern parade category.

## E53 — `西北臨水殿` / `面北臨水殿`

### Witness state

Kanripo / Siku-family text transmits:

> 入池門内南岸西去百餘歩有西北臨水殿……又西去數百歩乃仙橋

A substantial edited and scholarly tradition reads:

> 有面北臨水殿

### Spatial evidence

The `面北` reading has strong architectural coherence with the narrative sequence: the hall is placed on the **south bank** and faces the pool / water-stage area to the north. Modern reconstruction scholarship on the Jinming Pool likewise uses `面北臨水殿`, and archaeological discussion identifies a building foundation on the south shore as a likely Linshui Hall site.

### Research decision

For the project:

- **working spatial reading / translation: `面北臨水殿`**;
- **apparatus: Siku/Kanripo `西北臨水殿`**;
- mark this as an edited / architecturally coherent preferred reading, not as an invisible correction.

Future map label can therefore orient the hall as north-facing, but the variant must remain discoverable in the text detective panel.

### Guardrail

Do not claim that archaeology alone proves the transmitted graph should be `面`. The result is a convergence of spatial syntax, edited textual tradition, and archaeological reconstruction.

## E53 — `閑池` / `閉池`

### Witness state

Siku-family witness:

> 質庫不以幾日解下只至閑池便典没出賣

Edited tradition frequently reads:

> 質庫不以幾日解下只至閉池便典沒出賣

Entry 60 later explicitly says:

> 自三月一日至四月八日閉池

### External lexical evidence

The Revised Mandarin Chinese Dictionary's entry for `典沒` quotes the *Dongjing Meng Hua Lu* passage in the form:

> 只至閉池，便典沒出賣

This quotation is especially useful because `閉池` gives a clear deadline: goods pawned during the temporary Jinming season need not be redeemed until the pool closes; if still unredeemed, they may be forfeited / sold.

### Research decision

- Prefer **`閉池`** in the working text and translation.
- Preserve **`閑池`** as the Siku-family substantive variant.
- Interpret the sentence as a time-bound pawn / resale practice tied to the seasonal closure of the pool.

### Claim boundary

Safe:
- Meng links temporary commerce / pawn activity to the duration of the opened-pool season.

Avoid:
- inferring a formal statute, official grace period, or uniform citywide pawn rule from this sentence alone.

## E54 — `百姓卸在行人`

### Witness state

The phrase is stable enough textually:

> 虎頭船十隻……餘皆着青短衣長頂頭巾齊舞棹乃百姓卸在行人也

### Research problem

Later annotation does not converge on one secure social / occupational identification. Modern explanations sometimes connect the phrase to people formerly attached to a service / occupational category and later returned to civilian status; other readings try to understand `卸在行` as an occupational label. The evidence inspected in this pass is too weak to turn either interpretation into the main text.

### Research decision

Keep:

**`百姓卸在行人` — TEXT HIGH / MEANING LOW**

Translation strategy:

> Meng identifies the rowers with the historical label `百姓卸在行人`; its precise social or occupational meaning remains disputed.

Do not use `dockworkers`, `stevedores`, `demobilized soldiers`, or another precise modern class label unless a stronger primary / institutional source is found.

## E59 — `出九和合` / `任大頭快活三`

This crux can now be substantially resolved.

### Primary / legal comparison

Tang legal material already distinguishes the two terms in gambling context:

> 其停止主人，及出九，若和合者，各如之。

Song-period legal / institutional material continues to place `出九` and `和合` together as gambling-related roles. Other Song sources also use `出九和合` in contexts of prohibited gambling activity.

A Song miscellany further records the gambling expression `出九入十` in connection with `攤賭`.

### Secondary synthesis

Modern scholarship on Song gambling commonly explains:

- **出九**: a professional operator / person supplying or running the gambling setup;
- **和合**: a person who gathers, arranges, or brings players together.

The exact institutional mechanics should remain modestly phrased, but the evidence is strong enough to reject a fused lexical reading such as `出九和合` = one game or one winning combination.

### Punctuation decision

Meng's sentence should be read:

> 出九、和合，有名者任大頭、快活三之類，餘亦不數。

### Names

`任大頭` and `快活三` are best treated here as **names / nicknames of famous practitioners** associated with these gambling roles.

`快活三` also circulates elsewhere as a theatrical / tune-name expression, but that later / parallel usage does not identify this person with a tune or theatrical role.

### Translation boundary

Safe paraphrase:

> Professional gambling operators — those who ran/provided the play and those who gathered players — included well-known figures such as Ren Datou and Kuaihuo San.

Avoid modern labels such as:
- casino dealer;
- bookmaker;
- croupier;
- gambling promoter,

unless clearly presented as loose analogies rather than historical equivalences.

### Status

- `出九、和合`: **HIGH — resolved enough for page build**.
- `任大頭、快活三`: **MEDIUM-HIGH segmentation; biographical identity remains unknown**.

## Jinming Pool spatial evidence — archaeology / reconstruction notes

Modern architectural-history reconstruction adds useful constraints for Entries 53–56, but remains **COMPARATIVE / SECONDARY**.

Useful convergence:

- archaeological work describes the Jinming Pool site as broadly rectangular;
- a substantial foundation on the south shore has been interpreted as the likely Linshui Hall location;
