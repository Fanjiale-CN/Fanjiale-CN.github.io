## E52 — `𣘚腳`: later lexical gloss materially improves meaning security

### Primary text

Kanripo / Siku-family witness:

> 諸軍禁衛各成隊伍，跨馬作樂四出，謂之「𣘚腳」。其旗旌鮮明，軍容雄壯，人馬精銳，又別為一景也。

### Later lexicographic evidence

方以智《通雅》卷十二 explicitly glosses the term:

> 清明出遊曰𣘚腳。宋清明禁衛作樂四出曰𣘚腳，𣘚音靸。

This is a seventeenth-century lexical witness, far later than Meng, but unusually explicit: it identifies `𣘚腳` as a Qingming outing and cites the Song capital guard scene.

### Updated decision

- **graph / source text:** `𣘚腳` — HIGH in Siku-family witness.
- **edited normalization:** `摔腳` — plausible later/modern normalization; do not replace the source silently.
- **meaning:** `Qingming outing / going out at Qingming` — **MEDIUM-HIGH, later lexicographic evidence**.

Safe editorial phrasing:

> Meng calls the mounted Qingming outing of guard units `𣘚腳`; a later lexicographer explicitly glosses the term as going out for Qingming.

Safe observable description remains primary: units formed groups, rode out, made music and displayed bright banners / military appearance.

Still avoid:
- specific dance form;
- cavalry drill;
- folk dance;
- modern parade taxonomy.

Source trail:
- 方以智《通雅》卷十二, Chinese Text Project, `清明出遊曰𣘚腳`.
- Kanripo KR2k0111 卷七 for Meng's transmitted text.

## E52 — `黃胖`: earthen figurine lock

### Lexical / Song comparison

Taiwan MOE Revised Mandarin Dictionary defines `黃胖` as a clay figure / clay chubby child and quotes 葉紹翁《四朝聞見錄》.

The Song text itself says:

> 韓以春日宴族人於西湖，用土為偶，名曰「黃胖」。以線繫其首，累至數十人。遊人以為土宜。

### Decision

**HIGH:** `黃胖` is an earthen/clay figurine called *huangpang*.

Translation should prefer a broad object description:

> a clay figurine called *huangpang*

Do not force the exact iconography into `fat baby doll` unless a specific visual source is being discussed. The dictionary gloss is narrower than the secure Song statement `用土為偶`.

This also strengthens the Entry 52 reading of the return bundle as a mixture of food, flowers, playthings and locally purchased objects.

Sources:
- 葉紹翁《四朝聞見錄》戊集「黃胖詩」, Chinese Text Project.
- 教育部《重編國語辭典修訂本》, `黃胖`.

## E52 — `掉刀`: weapon type secure; Qingming toy interpretation remains secondary

### Song military evidence

《武經總要》 describes `掉刀` among military blades:

> 掉刀，刃首上闊，長柄，施鐏……此皆軍中常用。

### Decision

- **object type / historical term:** HIGH — a long-handled blade whose blade-head widens toward the end, with a ferrule on the handle.
- **Entry 52 returned object as miniature/toy:** MEDIUM — contextual / later annotation, not proven by Meng's wording alone.

The Qingming list contains food, flowers, `山亭戲具`, eggs/chicks and other goods. This makes a plaything-sized `掉刀` plausible, but the main translation should expose the uncertainty.

Safe wording:

> a `diaodao`-shaped object / blade (later commentators often understand the Qingming item as a toy)

Avoid depicting a full-size military weapon being casually carried as festival merchandise unless the page explicitly marks that as an unresolved literal possibility.

Source:
- 《武經總要》前集, Chinese Text Project.

## E53 — opening the Jinming Pool: access permission now strongly corroborated

### Meng

> 三月一日州西順天門外開金明池瓊林苑……雖禁從，士庶許縱賞，御史臺有榜不得彈劾。

### Near-contemporary corroboration

周煇《清波雜志》 records a remembered yellow placard at 宜秋門:

> 三月一日三省同奉聖旨開金明池，許士庶遊行，御史臺不得彈奏。

Zhou Hui then records a Southern Song old-capital poem recalling the placard and says he preserves the detail `以補夢華之遺`.

### Decision

**HIGH:** public/commoner visitation was an explicitly permitted feature of the Jinming Pool opening regime, and censorial action was specifically limited in the posted wording recorded by Zhou Hui.

This materially strengthens the punctuation / interpretation of Meng's clause.

Editorial use:

> Permission itself is part of the spectacle: the opened imperial landscape announces when commoners may enter and how that exceptional access is governed.

Guardrails:
- do not call the whole site an unrestricted public park;
- do not generalize the placard into permanent rights of access;
- later entries explicitly retain restricted palace / banquet spaces.

Sources:
- 周煇《清波雜志》, `三月一日三省同奉聖旨開金明池許士庶遊行御史臺不得彈奏`.
- 厲鶚《宋詩紀事》卷九十六 preserves the same anecdote and quotes the Dongjing passage.

## E53 — `西北臨水殿` / `面北臨水殿`: keep the split visible

New witness review reinforces that `西北` is not a trivial modern typo: Qing compilations quoting *Dongjing Meng Hua Lu* preserve `西北臨水殿`.

Modern architectural / archaeological reconstruction still makes `面北` highly coherent because the likely Linshui Hall site lies on the south side and would face north toward the pool.

### Decision unchanged but sharpened

- **source-facing transmitted reading:** `西北臨水殿` — historically attested.
- **preferred spatial reconstruction reading:** `面北臨水殿` — strong edited / architectural reading.
- **status:** TEXT VARIANT REAL; SPATIAL PREFERENCE STRONG; CERTAINTY NOT CLAIMED.

Do not erase either side.

## E54 — `百姓卸在行人`: best current identification remains porter / loading guild, but secondary

全漢昇《中國行會制度史》 directly discusses the passage and interprets `卸在行` as a guild of wharf or land porters whose main work was unloading / carrying goods, suggesting `卸在` may reflect `卸載`.

This is a substantial economic-history interpretation and fits Meng's use of visible occupational dress elsewhere, but an independent Song-period exact parallel for `卸在行` has not yet been located.

### Decision

- **text:** HIGH.
- **porter / loading-unloading guild interpretation:** MEDIUM.
- keep alternate explanations discoverable.

Safe main-text formulation:

> Meng identifies the tiger-head-boat rowers by the occupational/social label `百姓卸在行人`. Quan Hansheng interpreted `卸在行` as a loading-and-carrying workers' guild; the exact identification remains debated.

Avoid unqualified:
- `dockworkers`;
- `stevedores`;
- `demobilized soldiers`.

Source:
- 全漢昇《中國行會制度史》, early economic-history discussion of occupational dress and `卸在行`.

## E57 — `丁都賽`: comparative material evidence lock

The National Museum of China holds a Northern Song carved theatrical brick excavated at Yanshi, Henan:

- object: `“丁都賽”戲曲雕磚`
- date: Northern Song
- dimensions: 28.4 × 9.3 cm
- image: female performer in theatrical dress
- inscription: `丁都賽`

The museum explicitly connects the inscription with the performer named by Meng Yuanlao and describes Ding Dusai as a famous performer in late Northern Song Tokyo.

### Evidence status

**COMPARATIVE MATERIAL EVIDENCE — HIGH**

This is exceptionally valuable because the project has a transmitted personal name in Meng and a roughly contemporary material object carrying the same name.

Safe use:

> A Northern Song theatrical brick excavated at Yanshi bears the name `丁都賽`; the National Museum of China connects it with the celebrated performer recorded by Meng Yuanlao.

Guardrail:

Do not claim the brick depicts a specific performance described in Entry 57. It corroborates the name / performer tradition, not the exact Baojin Tower scene.

Sources:
- 中国国家博物馆, `“丁都賽”戲曲雕磚` collection page.
- 中国国家博物馆, `戲曲女明星——丁都賽` research article.
