DONGJING MENG HUA LU / 卷一

內諸司

Inner Departments

Meng Yuanlao compresses an entire palace bureaucracy into one dense list. Read functionally, it becomes a service map: writing and communication, diplomatic reception, security, treasuries, food and clothing, transport, medicine, gardens and specialist craft.

READ ENTRY ↓

05 / 內諸司

A single inventory of institutions inside the forbidden precinct. The categories below are Galok's editorial grouping, not Meng Yuanlao's own taxonomy.← READING ROOM

Neizhusi is short in prose and huge in implication. After the palace architecture of Entry 04, Meng lists the machinery that kept the inner court functioning. The interesting thing is the range: policy papers, foreign envoys, weapons, treasure, alcohol, medicine, clothing and the imperial garden all sit inside the same restricted administrative world.

1. The palace is full of offices, not empty grandeur.

PRIMARY TEXT / 內諸司05A / WRITING + ACCESS + RECEPTION
內諸司皆在禁中，如學士院、皇城司、四方館、客省、東西上閤門、通進司、內弓劍鎗甲軍器等庫、翰林司（茶酒局也）……
WORKING TRANSLATION

The inner departments were all located within the forbidden precinct. Meng begins with the Academy of Scholars, the Imperial City Office, the Four Directions Hostel and Guest Reception Office, the East and West Upper Gate offices, the office for transmitting documents, internal stores for bows, swords, spears, armor and other weapons, and the Hanlin Service — which he explicitly glosses here as the tea and wine bureau.

Wikisource / Volume I ↗Translation: Galok / working translation
GALOK READING

Writing, diplomatic reception, document transmission and weapons storage appear in one breath. The forbidden city is a political communications hub and a security zone at the same time.

Meng's parenthesis on the “Hanlin Service” matters. Here he is not naming the scholarly Hanlin Academy again; he identifies this unit as a tea-and-wine bureau. Similar names can hide very different functions inside the palace.

2. Treasury and domestic service sit beside high politics.

PRIMARY TEXT05B / TREASURE + SIX PALACE BUREAUS
……內侍省、入內內侍省、內藏庫、奉宸庫、景福殿庫、延福宮、殿中省六尚局（尚藥、尚食、尚輦、尚醞、尚舍、尚衣）……
WORKING TRANSLATION

The list continues with the Palace Attendants' departments, the Inner Treasury, Fengchen and Jingfu Hall stores, Yanfu Palace, and the Six Bureaus under the Palace Directorate: medicine, food, palanquins, fermented drink, accommodation and clothing.

History of Song / Palace Directorate ↗Institutional context: Songshi
GALOK READING

The six bureaus turn the emperor's daily life into administrative categories. Eating, taking medicine, moving in a palanquin, drinking, lodging and dressing all require staff, stores, procedures and accountability.

History of Song describes the Palace Directorate as responsible for imperial food, medicine, clothing, canopies, palanquins and accommodation, and gives the six bureaus matching functional responsibilities. Meng's compressed list is therefore a street-level inventory of a real institutional service system.

01 / POLICY + WRITING

學士院 · 通進司 · 天章等閣

Drafting, transmitting documents and preserving court texts or archival material.

02 / RECEPTION

四方館 · 客省 · 東西上閤門

Envoys, guests and controlled movement through court-facing reception systems.

03 / SECURITY

皇城司 · 軍器等庫

Imperial-city control and stored weapons inside the restricted precinct.

04 / TREASURY

內藏庫 · 奉宸庫 · 景福殿庫

Stores and treasuries supporting court property, valuables and palace consumption.

05 / DAILY SERVICE

六尚局 · 翰林司

Food, medicine, transport, drink, lodging, clothing, plus tea-and-wine service.

06 / SPECIALIST WORK

醫官局 · 後苑作 · 翰林書藝局

Medical care, garden work and specialist artistic or technical service.

3. The last offices reveal how many kinds of expertise the court absorbs.

PRIMARY TEXT05C / SPECIALIST SERVICES
……諸閤分、內香藥庫、後苑作、翰林書藝局、醫官局、天章等閣、明堂頒朔布政府。
WORKING TRANSLATION

Meng closes with various palace divisions, the inner aromatics and medicines store, the imperial garden workshop, a Hanlin calligraphy and arts bureau, the Medical Officials' Bureau, the Tianzhang and related pavilions, and the Mingtang office associated with promulgating the calendar and distributing governmental directives.

Wikisource / Volume I ↗Translation: Galok / working translation
GALOK READING

The court consumes expertise as much as it consumes goods. Doctors, calligraphic specialists, gardeners, storekeepers, document handlers and attendants all become part of the palace's permanent operating system.

This is why the phrase “inner departments” can mislead modern eyes. These offices are not a small domestic staff tucked behind state government. They are one layer of state capacity, concentrated around the emperor's body and access to him.

INSTITUTIONAL CHECK / SIX BUREAUS

History of Song describes the Palace Directorate as overseeing the Six Bureaus of Food, Medicine, Fermented Beverages, Attire, Accommodation and Palanquins, with each bureau responsible for the corresponding imperial service. That later institutional compilation supports reading Meng's list as functional bureaucracy rather than decorative nomenclature. Chinese Text Project / Songshi ↗

EDITORIAL GROUPING

The six functional boxes on this page are a modern reading aid. Meng Yuanlao gives a continuous list and does not divide the offices into these categories.

NAME TRAP / 翰林司

Meng explicitly says the 翰林司 here is a tea-and-wine bureau. It should not be silently merged with the scholarly 學士院 simply because both carry “Hanlin” in modern translation habits.
