DONGJING MENG HUA LU / 卷四

魚行

The city eats from three gates.

Volume IV ends at the water. Live fish came to market alive — shallow buckets, willow leaves strung through clear water — and each dawn three gates counted the city's intake in shoulder-loads: Xinzheng, Xishui, Wansheng, thousands of loads entering before the day began. In winter the Yellow River's cart-fish answered, at under a hundred cash the catty.

00 / THREE GATES, ONE INTAKE

The supply counted in shoulder-loads.

The entry names the three dawn gates together and counts their traffic as one figure: 如此生魚有數千簷入門 — thousands of 簷, each 簷 a load carried on a shoulder pole. The unit is labor, not fish: the gate sees carriers, and the market sees what they carry.

01 / THE COLD CHAIN

Alive at the stall.

The section's method clause is a small technical marvel: 賣生魚則用淺抱桶，以柳葉間串清水中浸 — shallow carry-buckets, willow leaves strung through, clear water. Three terms, one apparatus. The page reads it as the trade's cold chain: fish kept alive in transit, not preserved dead.

When the cold months came, the trade reached outward: 冬月即黃河諸遠處客魚來，謂之「車魚」 — guest fish from the far reaches of the Yellow River, arriving by cart, named for the vehicle that carried them. 1. The morning run.

PRIMARY TEXT39A / THE MORNING INTAKE
賣生魚則用淺抱桶，以柳葉間串清水中浸，或循街出賣，每日早惟新鄭門、西水門、萬勝門，如此生魚有數千簷入門。
WORKING TRANSLATION

Those who sold live fish used shallow carry-buckets, strung with willow leaves and immersed in clear water, and hawked them street by street. Each morning it was at Xinzheng Gate, the West Water Gate and Wansheng Gate — live fish entering the gates by the thousands of shoulder-loads.

GALOK READING

Every clause is an infrastructure claim in miniature. The bucket and the willow keep the catch alive — a cold chain built from vegetation and water. The word 或循街出賣 makes the street itself a retail surface. And the intake is counted in 簷, shoulder-loads — the same load-word the rental trades of Entries 31 and 34 priced around. Volume IV's last trade measures its market in the same unit as its first: one person, carrying.

2. The winter price.

PRIMARY TEXT39B / THE WINTER CAR-FISH
冬月即黃河諸遠處客魚來，謂之「車魚」，每斤不上一百文。
WORKING TRANSLATION

In the winter months guest fish came from the far reaches of the Yellow River — called cart-fish, after the carts that brought them — at under a hundred cash per catty.

GALOK READING

The trade's seasonality arrives in one clause: winter closes the local water, and the market answers with distance. 車魚 names the fish after the vehicle — the cart enters the commodity's name, the way the shoulder-pole entered the counting unit. And the price is deliberately modest: under a hundred cash the catty, a figure of the same order as Entry 32's hired horse. The volume that opened with an empress's dragon palanquin closes with a price any caller could pay.

One character, one family.

簷 ↔ 檐THE LOAD WORD

Gutenberg prints 數千簷; the Siku transcription prints 檐 — the same pairing Entry 31 collated on the vehicle term, where the site kept 檐. Here the page keeps the Gutenberg form for the load word and records both.

惟 ↔ 唯THE ONLY GATE LIST

The morning list is introduced with 惟 — "only these" — carried by both primary witnesses. The exclusivity is the text's own: three gates, and the entry names no fourth.

車魚NAMED FOR THE VEHICLE

All three witnesses agree on the term. The page reads it as the entry presents it — fish named for the cart — and does not speculate on species or route beyond the Yellow River the text names.

02 / VOLUME IV CLOSESVolume IV ends at the water.

Twelve entries ago this volume opened under an empress's dragon canopy and ran through the rental city — funerals priced, banquets contracted. It closes where the city actually eats: buckets strung with willow, three gates admitting thousands of shoulder-loads, and winter fish priced for anyone. Rank, rental, food — one market, read from the top down to the catty.
