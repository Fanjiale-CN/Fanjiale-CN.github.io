From the Chongning and Daguan eras onward, the arts in the capital's pleasure grounds: Zhang Tingsou, Meng Zishu, and the zhu-zhang direction of small song — Li Shishi, Xu Poxi, Feng Yinu, Sun Sansi and the rest, each the finest of their corner. Piaochang song disciples: Zhang Qiqi, Wang Jingnu, Zuo Xiaosi, An Niang, Mao Tuan and the others. Released from the court troupe and rehearsing: Zhang Cuigai, Zhang Cheng's disciples, Xue Zida, Xue Zixiao, Qiao Zhi'er, Yang Zongxi, Zhou Shounu, Chen Xin and the rest. Zaju-playing: the rod-puppeteer Ren Xiaosan, whose small zaju opened at the fifth watch daily — come later and you had missed it. String marionettes, Zhang Jinxian. Li Waining, gunpowder-effect puppets. Zhang Zhenmiao, Wen Nuge, Zhen'ge Qiang, Mo Boqi, Xiao Diaodao — acrobatics on ropes and mixed hands. Hunshen Yan, Li Zongzheng, Zhang Ge — ball-stick tricks. Sun Kuan, Sun Shiwu, Zeng Wudang, Gao Shu, Li Xiaoxiang — history-telling. Li Zao, Yang Zhongli, Zhang Shiyi, Xu Ming, Zhao Shiheng, Jia Jiu — story-telling. Wang Yanxi, Gai Zhongbao, Liu Minguang — varied music. Zhang Zhennu — spinning dance. Yang Wangjing — children's wrestling, zaju, knife-play, shield charts. Dong Shiwu, Zhao Qi, Cao Baoyi, Zhu Po'er, Mei Kunduo, Feng Sengge, Zu Liunong — shadow plays. Ding Yi, Shouji and the rest — comic shadow plays. Liu Baiqin — insect tricks. Kong Sanchuan, Shua Xiucai — zhugongdiao narrative song. Mao Xiang, Huo Bochou — riddles. Wu Ba'er — he-sheng duets. Zhang Shanren — comic tales. Liu Qiao, Hebeizi, Bo Sui, Hu Niu'er, Dayan Wu, Chongming Qiao, Luotuo'er, Li Dun and the rest — variety sketching. A guest turn by Sun San's spirits. Huo Sijiu — telling the Three Kingdoms. Yin Changmai — the Five Dynasties. Wen Baniang — fruit-crying calls. The rest cannot be counted.

GALOK READING

The register is the argument. No other passage in the book names this many private individuals, and none organizes them so like a trade directory: genre label, then roster. The page translates the whole list rather than excerpting stars, because the point is coverage — a market large enough that puppetry alone supported multiple named specialisms — rod, string and gunpowder effects — and a Three Kingdoms storyteller was specifically associated with that single saga.

2. The schedule, and the weather.

PRIMARY TEXT41B / TIME AND THE CROWDS
其餘不可勝數。不以風雨寒暑。諸棚看人，日日如是。教坊鈞容直，每遇旬休按樂，亦許人觀看。每遇內宴前一月，教坊內勾集弟子小兒，習隊舞，作樂雜劇節次。
WORKING TRANSLATION

The rest cannot be counted. Rain or shine, cold or heat did not matter: the spectators at the sheds were the same every day. The court ensembles Jiaofang and Junrongzhi played on their ten-day rest days, and the public was allowed to watch. And a month before each inner-court banquet, the Jiaofang assembled its disciples and children to rehearse the line-dances and the sequence of music and zaju.

GALOK READING

Three clauses turn a name list into a schedule: the fifth-watch opener, the ten-day court rehearsals open to onlookers, and the month-long assembly before palace banquets. Entertainment here is infrastructure — scheduled, rehearsed, weatherproofed — and the state's own performers rehearse where the public can watch. One scholarly reconstruction connects the passage's mention of troupe reductions with the court cutbacks of 1126; the page reports it as a hypothesis, not a date.

The Siku witness prints 枝頭傀儡 where common editions print 杖頭傀儡 (rod puppets). The working text keeps the common form and records the Siku variant here.

Gutenberg drops the insect character (弄蟻 only); the Siku witness prints 弄䖝蟻 — insect tricks — and 䖝 is the variant form of 蟲. The page restores it.

02 / THE REGISTER CLOSESEvery art had a name.

Entry 41 is the closest a twelfth-century text comes to a trade directory of show business — dozens of named performers across numerous genres, a schedule, and weather that did not matter. The page keeps the list whole and the disputes open, because the untidy parts are where the text is most itself.
