DONGJING MENG HUA LU / 卷四

會仙酒樓

The silver standard.

Volume IV turns from renting banquets to running them. At the great shops — Renhe, and the Huixian House proper shop inside the new gate — the service gear was never short by a single item. Whoever you were, even two people drinking opposite each other drew a standard set that priced out near a hundred taels of silver.

35 / 會仙酒樓

A tableware minimum that makes luxury standard: one set, near a hundred taels, whatever the party.↗ THE STANDARD SET↗ TEXT DETECTIVE

1. The shops that are never short.

PRIMARY TEXT35A / THE SHOP
如州東仁和店、新門裏會仙樓正店，常有百十分廳館，動使各各足備，不尚少闕一件。
WORKING TRANSLATION

Take the Renhe shop east of the prefecture, or the Huixian House proper shop inside the new gate: their hundred-fold halls and rooms were always fully equipped with movable service gear — never one item short.

GALOK READING

The section opens with 如 — "for instance" — because Entry 13 already surveyed the seventy-two proper shops. This page instantiates the standard through two named houses. What they stock is not food but 動使, the movable service gear, and the claim is completeness: 不尚少闕一件, not one piece short, ever.

2. The minimum, priced by the text itself.

PRIMARY TEXT35B / THE SILVER STANDARD
大抵都人風俗奢侈，度量稍寬，凡酒店中不問何人，止兩人對坐飲酒，亦須用注碗一副，盤盞兩副，果菜碟各五片，水菜碗三五隻，即銀近百兩矣。雖一人獨飲，碗遂亦用銀盂之類。其果子菜蔬，無非精潔。若別要下酒，即使人外買軟羊、龜背、大小骨、諸色包子、玉板鮓、生削巴子、瓜薑之類。
WORKING TRANSLATION

On the whole, the capital's customs were lavish and its measure generous. In any wine house it did not matter who you were: even two people sitting opposite each other over a drink required one pour-bowl set, two dish-and-cup sets, five plates each of fruit and dishes, three to five water dishes — silver coming to nearly a hundred taels. Even a man drinking alone had bowls of silver and the like. The fruit and vegetables were unfailingly fine. And if other food was wanted to go with the wine, a servant was sent out to buy soft lamb, turtle-back cakes, large and small bones, every kind of bun, jade-board pressed fish, raw sliced pork, and pickled ginger.

GALOK READING

The entry prices its own scene: 即銀近百兩矣 — the set on the table is worth near a hundred taels. The figure is the text's own estimate, and the page does not convert it. What the section documents is a standard, not a splurge: the minimum applies to anyone (不問何人), to a party of two, to a drinker alone. Meanwhile the hot kitchen is partly outsourced — the shop's own pride is the silver on the table, and the cooked dishes are bought in from the street.

02 / TEXT DETECTIVE

Three places the witnesses divide.

五片 ↔ 五斤PIECES OR CATTIES

Gutenberg and the simplified cross-check print 果菜碟各五片; the Siku transcription prints 五斤. The page keeps 片 and records 斤 — the plate count, not a weight.

裏 ↔ 裡ORTHOGRAPHY

The Siku witness prints 新門裏; Gutenberg prints 裡. The page keeps 裏, the standard traditional form.

碗遂亦用銀盂TRANSMITTED AS IS

All three witnesses print 遂. Read adverbially — even drinking alone, the bowls were then of silver — the sentence stands. The page keeps the transmitted text and emends nothing.

02 / THE FLOOR IS SILVERLuxury is inventory.

Entry 35 opens Volume IV's food block with a paradox: the most expensive thing in the room is not for sale. The great houses compete on gear that is simply present — complete, silver, and brought out for anyone who sits down. Volume IV moved from the court's vehicles to the rental city; here the rental city shows what it can put on a table.

即銀近百兩矣。
