12 / 潘樓東街巷

Four textual branches: predawn market, east toward Old Caomen, south through ovens and night trade, north toward the horse market.← READING ROOM

Meng does not give one clean avenue. The remembered district keeps splitting, and different uses overlap by both place and hour. The page therefore treats Entry 12 as a network rather than a modern map.

1. Before dawn, trade gathers under lamps.

PRIMARY TEXT12A / GHOST MARKET
潘樓東去十字街，謂之土市子，又謂之竹竿市。又東十字大街，曰從行裹角茶坊。每五更點燈，博易買賣衣物圖畫、花環領抹之類，至曉即散，謂之「鬼市子」。
WORKING TRANSLATION

East of Panlou was a crossroads called Tushizi, also known as the Bamboo-Pole Market. Farther east, at another crossroads, stood the Congxing Guojiao tea house. At every fifth watch lamps were lit and people traded clothing, pictures, flower garlands, neckbands and similar goods. At dawn the market dispersed; it was known as the “Ghost Market.”

Yuan-collation lock: 衣物圖畫Translation: Galok / working translation
GALOK READING

The source defines the “Ghost Market” operationally: lamps at the fifth watch, a particular group of goods, and dispersal at dawn. Nothing in this passage makes it an illegal or underground market.

2. Eastward, commerce shares the route with memory.

PRIMARY TEXT12B / EAST BRANCH
以東街北趙十萬宅，街南中山正店、東榆林巷、西榆林巷。北鄭皇后宅。東曲首向北，墻畔單將軍廟，乃單雄信墓也，上有棗樹，世傳乃棗槊發芽生長成樹，又謂之棗冢子巷。又投東則舊曹門街，北山子茶坊，內有仙洞、仙橋，士女往往夜遊喫茶於彼。又李生菜小兒藥鋪、仇防禦藥鋪。
WORKING TRANSLATION

Farther east, on the north side of the street, stood Zhao Shiwan’s residence; on the south were the Zhongshan principal wine house and East and West Yulin lanes. To the north stood the residence of Empress Zheng. Turning east and then north, beside the wall, was the shrine of General Shan, said here to be the tomb of Shan Xiongxin. A jujube tree grew above it; local tradition said it had sprouted from his jujube-wood lance, and the lane was therefore called Zaozhongzi Lane. Farther east was Old Caomen Street. Beishanzi Tea House contained an “Immortal Cave” and “Immortal Bridge,” and men and women often went there at night to drink tea. Nearby were Li Shengcai’s children’s-medicine shop and Qiu Fangyu’s medicine shop.

Text lock: 東榆林巷、西榆林巷 · legend flagged 世傳Translation: Galok / working translation
GALOK READING

The Shan Xiongxin tomb and lance-tree story is introduced as 世傳 — transmitted local memory. Galok keeps it visually separate from ordinary route evidence rather than presenting it as verified archaeology.

3. Southward, food production and nightlife intensify.

PRIMARY TEXT12C / SOUTH BRANCH
出舊曹門，朱家橋瓦子。下橋，南斜街、北斜街，內有泰山廟，兩街有妓館。橋頭人煙市井，不下州南。以東牛行街、下馬劉家藥鋪、看牛樓酒店，亦有妓館，一直抵新城。自土市子南去，鐵屑樓酒店、皇建院街、得勝橋鄭家油餅店，動二十餘爐，直南抵太廟街、高陽正店，夜市尤盛。
WORKING TRANSLATION

Outside Old Caomen stood the wazi at Zhu Family Bridge. Below the bridge were the South and North Slanting Streets, with a Mount Tai temple inside and houses of sex work on both streets. The bridgehead was densely inhabited and commercially active, rivaling the southern part of the prefectural city. Farther east were Niuxing Street, the Liu family medicine shop at Xiama, the Kanniulou wine house, and more houses of sex work, extending all the way to the new city wall. South from Tushizi were the Tiexielou wine house, Huangjianyuan Street, and the Zheng family oil-cake shop at Desheng Bridge, where more than twenty ovens were in operation. Continuing south led to Taimiao Street and Gaoyang Zhengdian, where the night market was especially busy.

Cross-volume note: Zheng shop is later described again with 50+ ovensTranslation: Galok / working translation
CROSS-VOLUME / 餅店

The same Zheng-family business near Huangjianyuan appears again in Volume IV, where Meng describes a leading baking shop with more than fifty ovens. The two numbers are preserved as separate observations rather than “corrected” into one.

4. Northward, the district reaches the horse market.

PRIMARY TEXT12D / NORTH BRANCH
土市北去，乃馬行街也，人煙浩鬧。先至十字街，曰鷯兒市，向東曰東雞兒巷，向西曰西雞兒巷，皆妓館所居。近北街曰楊樓街，東曰莊樓，今改作和樂樓，樓下乃賣馬市也。近北曰任店，今改作欣樂樓，對門馬鐺家羹店。
WORKING TRANSLATION

North from Tushizi was Maxing Street, crowded with residents and activity. The first crossroads was called Liao’er Market. To the east lay East Ji’er Lane and to the west West Ji’er Lane, both occupied by houses of sex work. Nearer the northern street was Yanglou Street; to the east stood Zhuanglou, later renamed Helelou, beneath which was the horse market. Farther north stood Rendian, later renamed Xinlelou, opposite the Ma Dang family soup shop.
