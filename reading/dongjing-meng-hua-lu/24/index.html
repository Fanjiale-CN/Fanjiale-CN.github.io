WORKING TRANSLATION

When people sought workers for hire — including categories such as gandang personnel, food-and-drink service and artisans — each category had hanglao who supplied workers. When seeking a female servant, the customer was directed or introduced to a yaren.

GALOK READING

Demand and labor meet through named intermediaries. The sentence is tiny, yet it reveals a city where finding service workers could be organized by occupational channel.

03 / LATER SOUTHERN SONG PARALLEL

《夢粱錄》 shows the same vocabulary in a much denser labor market.

Wu Zimu's account of Southern Song Lin'an has its own section titled 「雇覓人力」. Written for another capital and a later century, it dramatically expands the categories behind Meng Yuanlao's compressed Kaifeng note.

KAIFENG / 《東京夢華錄》

Compressed mechanism

幹當人、酒食、作匠 are supplied through 行老. Female servants are sought through 牙人.

LIN'AN / 《夢粱錄》卷十九

Expanded occupational catalogue

The later text names restaurant staff, cooks, porters, gardeners, pharmacy workers, household servants and many other positions, repeatedly placing 行老 in the matching process.

04 / TRUST + ACCOUNTABILITY

The Lin'an parallel adds a layer that Meng leaves unstated: who knows the worker.

《夢粱錄》 says that if a worker fled carrying goods, an original local contact or guarantor could go after them. It also records official and private female brokers and separate intermediaries for hiring travel porters.

05 / METHOD

A later parallel can clarify structure without being projected backward wholesale.

The two books share the section title 雇覓人力 and the vocabulary of 行老. That continuity makes comparison productive. Lin'an's much longer list also reflects its own time, institutions and urban conditions.

The comparison supports persistence and elaboration of labor intermediation across Song urban life. It does not prove that every Lin'an role, guarantee practice or brokerage category existed in exactly the same form in Meng Yuanlao's Kaifeng.

TERM LIMIT / INSTITUTIONSKEEP THE HISTORICAL NAMES.

行老 and 牙人 can be described by their intermediary function. Their full institutional, legal and gendered meanings need wider documentation than a modern job-market analogy supplies.
