02 / WHY THE STATE NEEDED MONEY

Take the government's answer as a claim, then check it against the wider record.← READING ROOM

The senior official's reply comes immediately. He does not defend the policies by saying that government should run profitable industries as a general principle. He gives a concrete reason: the frontier was expensive.

PRIMARY TEXT / OFFICIAL REPLYEXCERPT
邊用度不足，故興鹽、鐵，設酒榷，置均輸，蕃貨長財，以佐助邊費。
Frontier expenditures were insufficient. Therefore salt and iron were established, the alcohol monopoly was set up, and junshu was instituted, expanding goods and revenue in order to support frontier expenses.
《鹽鐵論·本議》 ↗Translation: Galok / provisional

The official case is a fiscal case.

Inside the text, the chain of reasoning is explicit: repeated conflict on the northern frontier required fortifications, beacon towers and garrison troops; those commitments created expenses; the revenue institutions were defended as a way to help pay those costs.

That explanation also fits the broad chronology we can establish outside the debate. Emperor Wu's reign brought a much more aggressive military posture toward the Xiongnu. Modern archaeological scholarship notes that central control over iron and salt was established in 119 BCE, decades before the 81 BCE conference. A Cambridge Business History Review article likewise summarizes the fiscal logic preserved in the debate: state control of salt and iron was defended as a source of revenue for campaigns against the Xiongnu.

TEXT CLAIM ≠ FINAL VERDICT

At this point we can say that frontier finance was the government's stated justification and that the chronology is compatible with that claim. We are not yet assigning a precise share of Han military spending to salt-and-iron revenue, and we are not treating the official speech as an audited budget statement.

This distinction matters for the whole Reading project. A historical text can tell us what an actor argued, feared or wanted to justify. It cannot, by itself, prove that the actor's causal story was complete. The next layer has to come from the wider fiscal record.
