READING / 001 OPENING NOTE 81 BCE / CHANG'AN

YANTIELUN / 鹽鐵論

When the state enters the market.

We are starting at the beginning. Before the arguments over salt, iron, war finance and merchants, the text records a simpler question: what were people suffering from?

BEGIN READING ↓

01 / THE FIRST QUESTION

Start with the text before building the argument.

Yantie Lun opens in the sixth year of the Shiyuan reign, 81 BCE. The court summons a discussion between senior officials and men recommended as xianliang and wenxue. The first line tells us what they were asked to discuss.

PRIMARY TEXT / 本議第一OPENING SENTENCE
惟始元六年，有詔書使丞相、御史與所舉賢良、文學語。問民間所疾苦。
In the sixth year of Shiyuan, an imperial order instructed the chancellor and the imperial secretary to confer with the recommended Worthies and Literati. They were asked what hardships people were suffering.
Digital transcription: Chinese Text Project ↗ Translation: Galok / provisional

The debate begins with hardship.

The wording matters. The recorded question is not framed as a technical inquiry into monopoly design or state revenue. It asks about minjian suo jiku — the hardships among the people. That framing gives the first response its direction.

PRIMARY TEXT / FIRST RESPONSEEXCERPT
今郡國有鹽鐵酒榷均輸，與民爭利。
Today the commanderies and kingdoms have salt and iron, the alcohol monopoly, and the junshu system, competing with the people for profit.
《鹽鐵論·本議》 ↗ junshu is left untranslated for now; the institution needs its own note.

That is the first accusation we need to understand. The critics place salt, iron, alcohol and junshu inside one complaint: government activity has crossed into fields where it now competes for profit. The officials answer immediately that these policies were tied to frontier defense and the fiscal burden inherited from Emperor Wu's reign. We will take that answer next, on its own terms.

WHAT WE KNOW AT THIS STAGE

The court debate is internally dated to 81 BCE. The opening problem is public hardship. The first policy dispute concerns whether revenue institutions built around salt, iron, alcohol and transport had become a burden on the people.

Why did the Han government build these institutions in the first place? The next section will reconstruct the fiscal and frontier setting before judging either side.
