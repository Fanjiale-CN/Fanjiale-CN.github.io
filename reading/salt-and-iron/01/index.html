Ben Yi — Original Debate

The whole opening chapter in one place: the court asks about hardship, the critics attack the profit-seeking state, the officials answer with frontier finance, and the argument ends inside the machinery of junshu and pingzhun.

01 / BEN YI

This page covers the complete first chapter rather than splitting it into separate thematic notes.← READING ROOM

Ben Yi is short enough to read in one sitting and dense enough to establish almost every major tension that the rest of Yantie Lun will keep returning to. The sequence matters. The chapter begins with hardship, moves to military finance, widens into a dispute over commerce and social order, then finishes with a remarkably concrete argument over how state purchasing and price stabilization actually work.

1. The conference begins with hardship.

PRIMARY TEXT / 本議第一OPENING EXCHANGE
惟始元六年，有詔書使丞相、御史與所舉賢良、文學語，問民間所疾苦。

文學對曰：「竊聞治人之道，防淫佚之原，廣道德之端，抑末利而開仁義，毋示以利，然後教化可興，而風俗可移也。今郡國有鹽、鐵、酒榷、均輸，與民爭利。散敦厚之樸，成貪鄙之化。是以百姓就本者寡，趨末者眾。夫文繁則質衰，末盛則本虧。末修則民淫，本修則民慤。民慤則財用足，民侈則飢寒生。願罷鹽、鐵、酒榷、均輸，所以進本退末，廣利農業，便也。」

大夫曰：「匈奴背叛不臣，數為寇暴於邊鄙，備之則勞中國之士，不備則侵盜不止。先帝哀邊人之久患，苦為虜所系獲也，故修障塞，飭烽燧，屯戍以備之。邊用度不足，故興鹽、鐵，設酒榷，置均輸，蕃貨長財，以佐助邊費。今議者欲罷之，內空府庫之藏，外乏執備之用，使備塞乘城之士飢寒於邊，將何以贍之？罷之，不便也。」
In the sixth year of Shiyuan, an imperial order instructed the chancellor and the imperial secretary to confer with the recommended Worthies and Literati and ask what hardships the people were suffering.

The Literati replied that good government should restrain the pursuit of secondary profits, cultivate benevolence and righteousness, and avoid teaching society to chase gain. They argued that the salt and iron system, the alcohol monopoly and junshu now placed the state in competition with the people for profit. In their view this weakened plain customs, drew people away from agriculture and encouraged luxury and acquisitiveness. They therefore asked that the policies be abolished so that agriculture and the “root” occupations could recover.

The senior official answered with the frontier. Xiongnu attacks had made defense expensive. Barriers, beacon towers and garrisons had to be maintained, and frontier expenditures exceeded ordinary resources. Salt, iron, the alcohol monopoly and junshu, he said, had been created to expand revenue and help pay those costs. Abolishing them would empty the treasury and leave soldiers on the border without adequate support.
Chinese Text Project ↗Translation: Galok / working translation

The opening establishes two incompatible ways of measuring the same institutions. The Literati begin from burden, incentives and social order. The official begins from state capacity and a budget constraint. One side asks what the policies do to ordinary economic life; the other asks what happens to defense if the revenue disappears.

The phrase “competing with the people for profit” therefore enters the book with several meanings already attached to it. It is an accusation about material burden, about the state occupying profitable channels, and about what elite behavior teaches the rest of society to value. Reading it as a simple modern argument over public versus private ownership would strip away most of the chapter.

2. Is the war itself the problem?

PRIMARY TEXTWAR AND GOVERNMENT
文學曰：「孔子曰：『有國有家者，不患寡而患不均，不患貧而患不安。』故天子不言多少，諸侯不言利害，大夫不言得喪。畜仁義以風之，廣德行以懷之。是以近者親附而遠者悅服。故善克者不戰，善戰者不師，善師者不陣。修之於廟堂，而折衝還師。王者行仁政，無敵於天下，惡用費哉？」

大夫曰：「匈奴桀黠，擅恣入塞，犯厲中國，殺伐郡縣、朔方都尉，甚悖逆不軌，宜誅討之日久矣。陛下垂大惠，哀元元之未贍，不忍暴士大夫於原野；縱然被堅執銳，有北面復匈奴之志，又欲罷鹽、鐵、均輸，憂邊用，損武略，無憂邊之心，於其義未便也。」

文學曰：「古者貴以德而賤用兵。孔子曰：『遠人不服，則修文德以來之；既來之，則安之。』今廢道德而任兵革，興師而伐之，屯戍而備之，暴兵露師，以支久長，轉輸糧食無已，使邊境之士飢寒於外，百姓勞苦於內。立鹽、鐵，始張利官以給之，非長策也。故以罷之為便也。」
The Literati answer that a ruler should worry about inequality and instability before simple scarcity. Humane government, they argue, wins attachment without relying on costly war; if distant peoples do not submit, moral rule should draw them in.

The official rejects that premise. The Xiongnu, he says, have crossed the frontier and killed officials and subjects. The threat is concrete, long-standing and punishable. A government that wants to defend the frontier cannot simultaneously dismantle the revenue institutions that support military preparedness.

The Literati then shift the argument one level upward. They say the recurring fiscal burden is itself produced by a strategy that privileges arms over moral government. Endless mobilization, garrison duty and grain transport leave soldiers exposed on the frontier and households exhausted at home. Creating profit-seeking offices to finance that cycle, they argue, is not a durable solution.
Wikisource / Volume I ↗Translation: Galok / working translation

This exchange is important because the two sides are now arguing over causation, not only morality. The official treats frontier expenditure as an external necessity. The Literati treat a large part of that expenditure as the consequence of a political choice: persistent military confrontation. A revenue institution can look necessary if the spending commitment is fixed; it can look pathological if the commitment itself is questioned.

3. What is commerce for?

PRIMARY TEXTROOT, BRANCH AND EXCHANGE
大夫曰：「古之立國家者，開本末之途，通有無之用，市朝以一其求，致士民，聚萬貨，農商工師各得所欲，交易而退。《易》曰：『通其變，使民不倦。』故工不出，則農用乏；商不出，則寶貨絕。農用乏，則穀不殖；寶貨絕，則財用匱。故鹽、鐵、均輸，所以通委財而調緩急。罷之，不便也。」

文學曰：「夫導民以德則民歸厚；示民以利，則民俗薄。俗薄則背義而趨利，趨利則百姓交於道而接於市。《老子》曰：『貧國若有餘。』非多財也，嗜欲眾而民躁也。是以王者崇本退末，以禮義防民欲，實菽粟貨財。市，商不通無用之物；工不作無用之器。故商所以通鬱滯，工所以備器械，非治國之本務也。」
The official argues that a functioning state must keep both “root” and “branch” activities open. Markets concentrate demand and goods; farmers, merchants and artisans exchange what each lacks. Without artisans, farmers lack tools. Without merchants, valuable goods do not circulate. Salt, iron and junshu, on this account, move stored resources and adjust shortages and urgent needs.

The Literati accept that merchants and artisans have functions, but deny that those functions should define government. Rule by virtue produces thick social bonds; public celebration of profit thins them. Markets should move what is genuinely useful and artisans should make necessary equipment, while the state should restrain luxury demand and keep agriculture in the primary position.
Chinese Text Project ↗Translation: Galok / working translation

The argument has now moved far beyond the original emergency. The official is defending circulation as an economic function in its own right. Production depends on tools; regions depend on exchange; shortages can be relieved through movement. The Literati concede much of that description and attack the hierarchy of ends: commerce may serve life, but it should not reorganize the values of the state.

4. Regional specialization or a machinery of desire?

PRIMARY TEXTMERCHANTS, ARTISANS AND REGIONAL GOODS
大夫曰：「《管子》云：『國有沃野之饒而民不足於食者，器械不備也；有山海之貨而民不足於財者，商工不備也。』隴、蜀之丹漆旄羽，荊、揚之皮革骨象，江南之楠梓竹箭，燕、齊之魚鹽旃裘，兗、豫之漆絲絺紵，養生送終之具也，待商而通，待工而成。故聖人作為舟楫之用，以通川谷，服牛駕馬，以達陵陸；致遠窮深，所以交庶物而便百姓。是以先帝建鐵官以贍農用，開均輸以足民財；鹽、鐵、均輸，萬民所戴仰而取給者，罷之，不便也。」

文學曰：「國有沃野之饒而民不足於食者，工商盛而本業荒也；有山海之貨而民不足於財者，不務民用而淫巧眾也。故川源不能實漏卮，山海不能贍溪壑。是以盤庚萃居，舜藏黃金，高帝禁商賈不得仕宦，所以遏貪鄙之俗，而醇至誠之風也。排困市井，防塞利門，而民猶為非也，況上之為利乎？傳曰：『諸侯好利則大夫鄙，大夫鄙則士貪，士貪則庶人盜。』是開利孔為民罪梯也。」
The official invokes Guanzi: fertile land cannot feed people if tools are lacking, and natural wealth cannot enrich them if merchants and artisans are absent. He lists the products of different regions—lacquer, hides, timber, bamboo, fish, salt, textiles—and argues that these goods become useful only through craft and exchange. Boats, pack animals and transport connect valleys, plains and distant resources. The iron offices, he says, provision agriculture; junshu makes goods available. These are institutions on which ordinary people depend.

The Literati reverse the diagnosis. If a fertile region is hungry, they say, commerce has displaced agriculture; if a resource-rich region remains poor, too much effort has gone into luxury crafts rather than basic needs. They invoke rulers who restrained visible wealth and merchant status to argue that elite behavior shapes popular behavior. If officials themselves pursue profit, the state opens a path through which greed travels downward.
Wikisource / Volume I ↗Translation: Galok / working translation

This is one of the chapter's sharpest collisions. The official sees specialization and exchange as technologies that turn scattered resources into usable wealth. The Literati see the same network as capable of multiplying wants, status competition and diversion from subsistence. Both sides understand that institutions change behavior; they disagree over which behavior deserves to be encouraged.

5. The debate finally reaches the machinery: junshu and pingzhun.

PRIMARY TEXTDESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION
大夫曰：「往者，郡國諸侯各以其方物貢輸，往來煩雜，物多苦惡，或不償其費。故郡國置輸官以相給運，而便遠方之貢，故曰均輸。開委府於京師，以籠貨物。賤即買，貴則賣。是以縣官不失實，商賈無所貿利，故曰平準。平準則民不失職，均輸則民齊勞逸。故平準、均輸，所以平萬物而便百姓，非開利孔而為民罪梯者也。」

文學曰：「古者之賦稅於民也，因其所工，不求所拙。農人納其獲，女工效其功。今釋其所有，責其所無。百姓賤賣貨物，以便上求。間者，郡國或令民作布絮，吏恣留難，與之為市。吏之所入，非獨齊、阿之縑，蜀、漢之布也，亦民間之所為耳。行奸賣平，農民重苦，女工再稅，未見輸之均也。縣官猥發，闔門擅市，則萬物並收；萬物並收，則物騰躍；騰躍，則商賈侔利；自市，則吏容奸。豪吏富商積貨儲物以待其急，輕賈奸吏收賤以取貴，未見準之平也。蓋古之均輸，所以齊勞逸而便貢輸，非以為利而賈萬物也。」
The official gives the chapter's clearest institutional explanation. In the past, commanderies and kingdoms sent local products as tribute, but transport was cumbersome and sometimes cost more than the goods were worth. Transport offices were therefore created to coordinate movement and ease long-distance tribute; this was called junshu, “equalized transport.” Warehouses in the capital then gathered commodities. When prices were low the government bought; when prices were high it sold. This was pingzhun, “price stabilization.” The official claims that the two systems reduced unequal transport burdens, protected occupations and denied merchants easy speculative profit.

The Literati attack the gap between design and administration. Taxation should take what people are good at producing, they say, rather than demand what they do not have. In practice households may be forced to sell cheaply to meet official demands; local authorities may order cloth and padding to be produced, delay transactions, and effectively tax household labor twice. When the state enters markets in bulk, prices can jump. Officials gain opportunities for favoritism and fraud, while wealthy merchants and corrupt functionaries can stockpile goods, buy cheaply and sell dear. The critics therefore say they have seen little of the promised “equalization” or “stabilization.” Older tribute equalization, they insist, was meant to distribute transport burdens, not turn government into a trader in everything.
Chinese Text Project ↗Translation: Galok / working translation

This final exchange is where Ben Yi becomes unusually modern in its institutional detail. The official presents a clean mechanism: coordinate logistics, buy low, sell high, stabilize prices, reduce speculative rents. The critics respond with implementation failure: compelled procurement, administrative discretion, price effects, corruption and private actors learning to arbitrage the state's own interventions.

The disagreement cannot be reduced to “state versus market.” The official is trying to use state capacity to correct transport and price problems. The critics are asking what happens when that capacity is exercised by actual officials with information gaps, bargaining power and opportunities for private gain. The chapter ends with the design still intact on paper and its execution under attack.

WHAT BEN YI ESTABLISHES

The first chapter gives the whole book its grammar: public hardship, fiscal pressure, war, agriculture, commerce, regional exchange, state purchasing, price stabilization and administrative abuse. Later chapters will take these pieces apart. Here they still appear as one connected dispute over what government is allowed to do when it claims necessity.
