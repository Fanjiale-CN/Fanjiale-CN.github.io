YANTIE LUN

Tong You — Circulation

通有第三

The third chapter moves from production to geography. Why are some cities rich without farming much themselves? Does exchange spread abundance, or does it merely concentrate desire, luxury and profit around the best-positioned places?

READ CHAPTER ↓

03 / TONG YOU

The complete third chapter. The debate turns to urban wealth, regional specialization and whether circulation solves scarcity or magnifies appetite.← READING ROOM

Tong You is the chapter where geography enters the argument as an economic force. The official side points to wealthy cities sitting on roads, markets and transport corridors. The Literati answer that location can produce bustle without durable household security. The dispute is therefore less about whether trade exists than about what kind of prosperity trade actually creates.

1. The official treats location as productive capital.

PRIMARY TEXT / 通有第三03A / CITY AND POSITION
大夫曰：「燕之涿、薊，趙之邯鄲，魏之溫軹，韓之滎陽，齊之臨淄，楚之宛、陳，鄭之陽翟，三川之二周，富冠海內，皆為天下名都，非有助之耕其野而田其地者也，居五諸之衝，跨街衢之路也。故物豐者民衍，宅近市者家富。富在術數，不在勞身；利在勢居，不在力耕也。」
WORKING TRANSLATION

The official lists famous cities from Yan, Zhao, Wei, Han, Qi, Chu, Zheng and the Zhou region. Their wealth, he argues, does not come from cultivating more fields than everyone else. They sit where roads converge and traffic passes. Where goods are abundant, population grows; households near markets prosper. Wealth can therefore come from position, calculation and access to exchange rather than from physical labor alone.

Chinese Text Project ↗Translation: Galok / working translation
GALOK READING

This is the book's clearest statement that geography itself can generate economic rent. A city at a transport junction gains from flows it did not physically produce. Information, brokerage, warehousing and proximity to buyers become productive advantages.

The phrase 勢居 is doing heavy work. The official side is saying that position can substitute for labor. That observation is uncomfortable for an agrarian moral hierarchy because it means two households can work equally hard and still face radically different opportunities simply because one sits closer to a network.

Once that is admitted, policy cannot think only in terms of fields and output. Roads, market access and the distribution of exchange points begin to shape wealth before any individual transaction takes place.

2. The Literati answer with a distinction between bustle and security.

PRIMARY TEXT03B / TRAFFIC WITHOUT RESERVES
文學曰：「荊、揚南有桂林之饒，內有江、湖之利，左陵陽之金，右蜀、漢之材，伐木而樹穀，燔萊而播粟，火耕而水耨，地廣而饒財；然民呰窳偷生，好衣甘食，雖白屋草廬，歌謳鼓琴，日給月單，朝歌暮戚。趙、中山帶大河，纂四通神衢，當天下之蹊，商賈錯於路，諸侯交於道；然民淫好末，侈靡而不務本，田疇不修，男女矜飾，家無斗筲，鳴琴在室。是以楚、趙之民，均貧而寡富。宋、衛、韓、梁，好本稼穡，編戶齊民，無不家衍人給。故利在自惜，不在勢居街衢；富在儉力趣時，不在歲司羽鳩也。」
WORKING TRANSLATION

The Literati point to regions rich in timber, metals, waterways and trade routes yet accuse many households there of living from day to day. Zhao and Zhongshan, they say, sit on major routes crowded with merchants and travelers, but fields are neglected and households lack reserves. By contrast, places they associate with farming and frugality are presented as more broadly secure. Their conclusion is that durable wealth comes from saving, labor and timely cultivation rather than from location alone.

Wikisource / Volume I ↗Translation: Galok / working translation
GALOK READING

The critics separate aggregate activity from household balance sheets. A city can look rich because goods, merchants and money are constantly moving through it while ordinary households remain one bad month away from distress.

That is a surprisingly modern warning. High transaction volume does not tell us how gains are distributed, how much wealth is retained locally or whether households build reserves. A transport hub may capture rents for landlords, merchants and brokers while exposing everyone else to high consumption pressure.

The Literati overstate the moral contrast between the industrious countryside and the frivolous city, but their accounting question survives the rhetoric: who actually owns the prosperity visible in a prosperous place?

3. The official turns regional difference into a case for circulation.

PRIMARY TEXT03C / UNEVEN GEOGRAPHY
大夫曰：「五行東方木而丹章有金銅之山，南方火而交趾有大海之川，西方金而蜀、隴有名材之林，北方水而幽都有積沙之地，此天地所以均有無而通萬物也。今吳、越之竹，隋、唐之材，不可勝用，而曹、衛、梁、宋采棺轉尸；江湖之魚，萊黃之鮐，不可勝食，而鄒、魯、周、韓藜藿蔬食。天下之利無不贍，而山海之貨無不富也，然百姓匱乏，財用不足，多寡不調，而天下財不散也。」
WORKING TRANSLATION

The official describes a world in which useful resources are scattered unevenly. Some regions have excess timber or bamboo while others lack material even for coffins; some waters contain abundant fish while inland communities live on sparse vegetables. The problem, he argues, is not that the realm lacks resources. Plenty and shortage sit in different places. Wealth remains concentrated because goods are not circulating widely enough.

Chinese Text Project ↗Translation: Galok / working translation
GALOK READING

This is the spatial version of the buffer-stock argument from Chapter 02. There the state moved abundance through time; here exchange moves abundance across geography.

The official's diagnosis is brutally simple: scarcity can be local even when aggregate supply is sufficient. That makes transport, market integration and information part of the provisioning system. A pile of fish beside one lake does nothing for a hungry household hundreds of kilometers away until some institution moves it.

The line 天下財不散 — wealth does not disperse through the realm — also exposes the political ambition behind the policy. Circulation is not valued merely because merchants earn money. It is presented as a way to flatten geographical inequality in access to goods.

4. The critics attack the demand side: distance can also manufacture waste.

PRIMARY TEXT03D / LUXURY AS MISALLOCATION
文學曰：「古者采椽不斲，茅屋不翦，衣布褐，飯土鉶，鑄金為鉏，埏埴為器，工不造奇巧，世不寶不可衣食之物，各安其居，樂其俗，甘其食，便其器，是以遠方之物不交而昆山之玉不至。今世俗壞而競於淫靡，女極纖微，工極技巧，雕素樸而尚珍怪，鑽山石而求金銀，沒深淵求珠璣，設機陷求犀象，張網羅求翡翠，求蠻貊之物以眩中國，徙邛筰之貨致之東海，交萬里之財，曠日費功，無益於用。是以褐衣匹婦勞罷力屈而衣食不足也。故王者禁溢利，節漏費；溢利禁則反本，漏費節則民用給，是以生無乏資，死無轉尸也。」
WORKING TRANSLATION

The Literati contrast a frugal ideal with a society chasing rare objects across great distances. In their account, artisans push technique toward ornament, miners and divers search for precious materials, and traders move exotic goods thousands of li for display rather than use. The problem is not simply transport cost. Luxury demand redirects labor and time away from ordinary clothing, food and tools. They therefore call for limiting excessive profit and wasteful expenditure.

Wikisource / Volume I ↗Translation: Galok / working translation
GALOK READING

This is the strongest answer to the claim that more circulation is automatically better. A network can move necessities toward shortages, and the same network can burn enormous resources satisfying status demand.

The critics are therefore asking a demand-composition question. What is the transport system carrying, and whose preferences decide? If scarce labor and logistics are consumed by prestige goods, the existence of a highly connected market can coexist with shortages of basic goods.

The weak point is obvious: usefulness cannot be decided cleanly from above. Yet the passage identifies a real externality. Private willingness to pay does not always reflect the social opportunity cost of the resources mobilized to satisfy that demand.

5. The official defends craft and trade as the machinery that makes abundance usable.

PRIMARY TEXT03E / THE NETWORK ITSELF
大夫曰：「古者宮室有度，輿服以庸，采椽茅茨，非先王之制也。君子節奢刺儉，儉則固……管子曰：『不飾公室，則材木不可勝用；不充庖廚，則禽獸不損其壽；無味利，則本業所出；無黼黻，則女紅不施。』故工商梓匠，邦國之用，器械之備也，自古有之……語曰：『百工居肆以成其事，農商交易以利本末。』山居澤處，蓬蒿墝埆，財物流通有以均之，是以多者不獨衍，少者不獨饉。若各居其處，食其食，則是橘柚不鬻，朐鹵之鹽不出，旃罽不市，而吳、唐之材不用也。」
WORKING TRANSLATION

The official rejects extreme frugality. Properly bounded consumption supports craftsmen, tools and useful production. Farmers and merchants exchange so that both primary and secondary occupations can function; goods move between mountains, wetlands and poor soils so that surplus in one place offsets shortage in another. Without exchange, citrus, salt, textiles and timber would remain trapped where they were produced.

Chinese Text Project ↗Translation: Galok / working translation
GALOK READING

The official side now makes its best systemic argument. Trade is infrastructure. It is the mechanism that turns local abundance into general availability.

That matters because production and access are different problems. A society can produce enough timber, salt or food in aggregate and still fail if each resource remains stranded behind distance. Exchange reduces the penalty imposed by geography.

The passage also refuses the idea that every non-agricultural occupation is parasitic. Craftsmen convert raw material into tools; merchants connect mismatched surpluses and deficits. The economy is described as a network of interdependence rather than a hierarchy with farming doing all meaningful work.

6. The final reply says the bottleneck is appetite, not nature.

PRIMARY TEXT03F / SCARCITY CREATED BY EXCESS
文學曰：「孟子云：『不違農時，穀不可勝食；蠶麻以時，布帛不可勝衣也；斧斤以時入，材木不可勝用；佃漁以時，魚肉不可勝食。』若其飾宮室，增臺榭，梓匠斲巨為小，以圓為方，上成雲氣，下成山林，則材木不足用也；男子去本為末，雖雕文刻鏤以象禽獸，窮物究變，則穀不足食也；婦女飾微治細以成文章，極技盡巧，則絲布不足衣也；庖宰烹殺胎卵，燔炙齊和，窮極五味，則魚肉不足食也。當今世，非患禽獸不損、材木不勝，患僭侈之無窮也；非患無旃罽橘柚，患無狹廬糟糠也。」
WORKING TRANSLATION

The Literati close by arguing that nature can provide enough when production respects timing and use remains restrained. Timber becomes scarce when architecture consumes it extravagantly; grain becomes scarce when labor leaves farming for ornament; cloth and meat become scarce when technique and appetite keep escalating. Their claim is that the central shortage of the age is not exotic goods but basic shelter and food for ordinary people.

Wikisource / Volume I ↗Translation: Galok / working translation
GALOK READING

Tong You ends with two competing explanations for scarcity. The official sees a distribution failure: resources exist but do not move. The Literati see an allocation failure: resources move, but too much of the system is organized around high-status consumption.

Those explanations can coexist. A region may lack goods because transport is weak while another household lacks them because purchasing power and productive capacity have been diverted elsewhere. The chapter never resolves that tension, which is exactly why it remains useful.

The sharper question left behind is concrete: when a network becomes more efficient at moving goods, what kind of demand does that efficiency serve first?

CHAPTER STOP / 03

Tong You turns the debate into a map. Prosperity depends on where people sit, what can move, and whether circulation is carrying necessities or amplifying luxury.
