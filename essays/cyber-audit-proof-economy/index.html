Viral desire enters the proof economy.

Under the cyber audit: after the influencer boom

察 Observe 2026 9 min read

Foreign observers often read China's platform internet as a question of speed: faster apps, faster virality, faster buying. The deeper shift is psychological. The social internet has trained consumers to defend themselves before they spend.

The next stage is not only consumption downgrade or value for money. It is cyber audit: an informal verification system made by consumers, platforms, merchants and local governments when every promise becomes visible in real time.

Cyber audit does not make consumers cold. It makes them harder to fool.

The platform became the consumer operating system

Platforms are no longer just marketing channels. They are where desire is seen, searched, compared, tested and sometimes rejected before money changes hands. The same feed that creates the myth also gives consumers tools to break the myth.

Platform economy is not influencer economy

The platform economy is the infrastructure: apps, algorithms, payments, logistics, rankings, data and moderation. The influencer economy is the attention layer on top. It turns visibility into trust, but trust now has to survive inspection.

Sometimes the face of attention is a livestreamer. Sometimes it is a restaurant. Sometimes it is an entire city. Zibo was not only selling barbecue. Harbin was not only selling winter. Tianshui was not only selling malatang. Each became a character in the feed: warm, cinematic, affordable, local, spicy, hospitable, worth photographing.

Viral cities have to behave like service brands

When a city goes viral, the product is no longer only food or scenery. The product is public belief: that visitors will not be cheated, that prices will stay legible, that crowds can be handled, and that the place can respond when the feed starts judging it.

The same machine can destroy trust

The platform does not only amplify beauty. It amplifies mismatch. If the photo is better than the place, users say so. If hotels raise prices too aggressively, users say so. If a destination looks like sincerity in one video and a trap in the next, the public mood turns.

That is why local governments are now part of the consumer experience. A complaint can become a city-brand crisis; a warm gesture can become a tourism asset; a price dispute can become a referendum on whether a place deserves trust.

Consumers still want emotion, but with protection

The mistake is to read cyber audit as the death of desire. Chinese consumers still want novelty, scenery, comfort, belonging, status, memory and reasons to leave the house. The success of viral cities proves that emotional narratives still work.

What changed is the defensive layer around emotion. Consumers want to feel moved without feeling fooled. They want to join the trend without becoming the sucker. They want the feeling, but they also want evidence that the feeling will not become regret.

The next stop is the proof economy

Under cyber audit, China's consumer market is not moving toward pure low price, pure virality or the end of emotional value. It is moving toward a proof economy.

Cyber audit as self-defense

Cyber audit connects directly to consumption audit. The consumer is not only asking whether something is cheap. The consumer is asking whether the photos are real, the queue is worth it, the price is stable, the city can handle the crowd, and the claim can survive the internet.

The old question was whether a brand or city could create desire. The new question is whether it can protect desire after the internet inspects it.
